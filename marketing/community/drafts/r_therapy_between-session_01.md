Therapy is great but it's one hour a week (or biweekly for many of us). The other 167 hours, I'm on my own with my brain. For a long time I treated therapy like a standalone event — went, talked, left, forgot until next time. Not much changed.

What shifted things: actually using tools between sessions to practice what we work on. Not in a rigid homework way, but having something accessible for the moments that matter — when I'm spiraling, when I'm avoiding, when I need to do a thought record before I lose the thought.

Things that have helped:
- Keeping thought records on my phone so I can do them in the moment
- Logging brief notes about what came up during the week so I remember what to bring up in session
- Having my grounding exercises accessible for bad moments
- A simple mood note a few times a week so I'm not relying on memory to tell my therapist how the week actually went

The difference: I come to sessions with actual material instead of "uh, the week was fine I guess?" and we can work on real things instead of reconstructing the week from memory.

What works for you between sessions? Do you use any tools or do you prefer to just live between sessions?
