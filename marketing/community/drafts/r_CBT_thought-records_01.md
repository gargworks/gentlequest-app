I'm a big believer in thought records — they're probably the single most useful CBT technique I've practiced. But I've noticed that most apps that include them overcomplicate the format. 10 columns, dropdown menus, rating scales, mood tags. By the time you've filled out the metadata, you've lost the thread of the actual thought.

The version that works for me is stripped down:
1. The situation (one line)
2. The automatic thought (what went through my mind)
3. Evidence for it
4. Evidence against it
5. A balanced alternative

That's it. Five fields. I can fill it out in 2-3 minutes when I'm spiraling, which is the only time it matters. If it takes longer than that, I won't do it, and then the technique is useless.

I also don't force myself to find "evidence against" if I genuinely can't. Sometimes just writing down the thought and the "evidence for" column reveals that the case is based on one flimsy thing. The balanced alternative sometimes writes itself once you see the evidence laid out.

Anyone else have a simplified thought record format they prefer? I'm curious how others handle the friction problem.
