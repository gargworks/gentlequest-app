I see a lot of posts here from people who want to get better but feel paralyzed by how much there is to do. Therapy, exercise, journaling, sleep schedule, socializing, routines — it's a mountain and you can't even get out of bed.

What helped me was abandoning the idea of "getting better" as a project and instead just doing one small honest thing per day. Not a habit tracker. Not a 30-day challenge. Just: what's one thing I can do today that future-me won't regret?

Some days that was drinking a glass of water. Some days it was opening the curtains. Some days it was texting one person back. The key was no judgment about whether it was "enough." Enough doesn't exist when you're in it.

I started writing these down — not to track progress or build a streak, just to remember that I'd done *something* on days when my brain insisted I'd done nothing. Looking back at that list on bad days was surprisingly powerful. Not because it proved I was "improving" but because it contradicted the depression narrative that I'm completely useless.

If you're at the bottom right now: you don't have to build a recovery plan. Just do one small thing. Then maybe another tomorrow. That's it.
