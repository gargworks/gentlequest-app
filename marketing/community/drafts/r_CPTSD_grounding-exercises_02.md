Dissociation is one of the hardest parts of CPTSD for me. When it hits, I'm not "anxious" in the obvious way — I'm just... not there. Floaty, disconnected, watching myself from a distance. And by the time I notice it's happening, I'm already pretty far gone.

The grounding techniques that work for me (your mileage may vary):

- **5-4-3-2-1 senses**: Name 5 things you see, 4 you hear, 3 you can touch, 2 you smell, 1 you taste. Classic but it works if I actually do it.
- **Temperature**: Cold water on my face or holding an ice cube. The shock brings me back into my body fast.
- **Physical pressure**: Pushing my feet into the floor, pressing my palms together hard, or a weighted blanket.

The problem was never not knowing these. It was remembering to do them when dissociating, because dissociation specifically makes it hard to initiate action. So I started keeping these written down in an app on my phone, openable in two taps. When I feel the floaty feeling starting, I open the list and just follow it mechanically. I don't have to remember or decide — just execute.

It doesn't always work fully. But even coming back 50% is better than staying gone.
