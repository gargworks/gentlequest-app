# Alternative to How We Feel: Mood Tracking With More Privacy and Less Complexity

If you're searching for an "alternative to How We Feel," you've likely experienced the strengths and limitations of emotion-granularity-focused mood tracking. How We Feel, developed with input from researchers including Marc Brackett of the Yale Center for Emotional Intelligence, is a thoughtful app that helps users identify nuanced emotions. But it's not the right fit for everyone. This article explores what people commonly want from a How We Feel alternative and what to consider when choosing one.

## What How We Feel Does Well

How We Feel brings emotional granularity — the ability to distinguish between closely related emotions — to a broad audience. Its strengths include:

- A rich emotion vocabulary organized by energy and pleasantness, helping users move beyond "good/bad" to "frustrated vs. anxious vs. overwhelmed"
- A beautiful, color-coded interface that makes emotional awareness visual
- Research-backed design informed by emotional intelligence frameworks
- Video strategies for managing difficult emotions
- A free model supported by a nonprofit partnership

For people who want to develop a richer emotional vocabulary and understand the nuances of what they're feeling, How We Feel is a genuinely valuable tool. The question is whether its approach and data model suit your needs over time.

## Why People Look for an Alternative

### The Complexity Ceiling

How We Feel's strength — emotional granularity — is also its limitation for some users. When you're overwhelmed, exhausted, or in crisis, being asked to choose between "tense," "anxious," "worried," "nervous," and "overwhelmed" can feel like too much. Sometimes "bad" or a simple 1–5 is all you can manage. The richness that's helpful on a good day can be paralyzing on a hard one.

### The Data and Account Model

How We Feel requires an account and syncs data to the cloud, including for research partnerships. While this is framed as contributing to emotional intelligence research — a noble goal — it means your mood and emotion data lives on servers you don't control. For privacy-conscious users, especially those logging sensitive emotional states, this is a meaningful consideration.

### The Research Participation Aspect

How We Feel's data sharing with researchers is transparent and opt-in in principle, but the app's model is built around data contribution. If your primary goal is personal mental health support rather than contributing to research, the data-sharing architecture may feel like an unnecessary exposure.

### The Scope

How We Feel focuses on emotion identification and regulation strategies. It doesn't typically include validated clinical screening tools (PHQ-9, GAD-7), grounding techniques for acute anxiety, an on-device journal for freeform processing, or crisis resources integrated into the flow. If you want a broader mental health toolkit, the emotion-focused approach can feel narrow.

### The Visual Overwhelm

The color-coded emotion grid is beautiful and informative, but for some users — particularly those with visual sensitivities or who find complex interfaces overwhelming during distress — the richness of the interface becomes a barrier rather than a help.

## What People Often Want Instead

### Simplicity on Hard Days

A mood check-in that can be as simple as one tap — a basic mood, no taxonomy required — but with the option to add more detail when you have the capacity. Simple by default, rich when you want it. Not the other way around.

### Privacy by Default

Mood logs, journal entries, and screening results stored on your device. No account required. No data shared with research partners unless you explicitly choose it. Your emotional data is your data.

### A Broader Toolkit

Mood tracking paired with validated screening tools, grounding and breathing techniques for acute moments, an on-device journal for real processing, and crisis resources one tap away. Emotion tracking is one piece; a full toolkit is more.

### Room for Freeform Reflection

Alongside structured mood logging, the ability to write freely — a few sentences or a few paragraphs — when you want to process something. The emotion label and the journal entry should live together, not in separate systems.

### No Research Data Sharing

An app that exists to serve you, not to collect data for studies. If research participation is something you want, it should be a deliberate choice — not the default architecture.

## How to Evaluate a How We Feel Alternative

### Questions to Ask

- **How complex is the mood entry?** Can you log in one tap on a hard day, or does it always require navigating a rich taxonomy?
- **Where does your data live?** On-device by default, or cloud-synced?
- **Is there an account requirement?** Can you track without signing up?
- **Does it share data with researchers or third parties?** Is this opt-in or structural?
- **Does it include screening tools, grounding techniques, and crisis resources?**
- **Does it support freeform journaling alongside mood tracking?**
- **Are there ads in the free version?**
- **Is the interface manageable when you're overwhelmed?**

### The Trade-off

How We Feel's emotional granularity framework is genuinely valuable for developing self-awareness and emotional intelligence. If your goal is to build a richer emotional vocabulary and you're comfortable with the data model, it's a strong choice. The question is whether you need something simpler, more private, and broader in scope — especially for the days when nuance is too much to ask of yourself.
