# Free GAD-7 Test Online: Understanding the Anxiety Screening Tool

Searching for a "free GAD-7 test online" usually means one thing: you want a quick, structured way to check whether the worry, restlessness, or tension you've been carrying is worth paying attention to. The Generalized Anxiety Disorder-7 (GAD-7) is the most common screening tool for exactly that. This article explains what it measures, walks through the seven questions, breaks down the scoring, and helps you understand what to do with the result.

## What the GAD-7 Measures

The GAD-7 is a seven-item self-report questionnaire that screens for generalized anxiety disorder and, more broadly, the severity of anxiety symptoms over the past two weeks. It was developed as a brief, validated companion to the PHQ-9 (which screens for depression). Clinicians use it in primary care, therapy intake, and research because it's fast, reliable, and easy to score by hand.

It doesn't distinguish between every type of anxiety disorder — it won't tell you whether you have panic disorder versus social anxiety versus GAD. What it does is give you a number that reflects how much anxiety has been interfering with your life recently.

## The Seven Questions

Over the last two weeks, how often have you been bothered by the following problems?

1. Feeling nervous, anxious, or on edge
2. Not being able to stop or control worrying
3. Worrying too much about different things
4. Trouble relaxing
5. Being so restless that it's hard to sit still
6. Becoming easily annoyed or irritable
7. Feeling afraid as if something awful might happen

Each item uses the same four-point scale: not at all = 0, several days = 1, more than half the days = 2, nearly every day = 3. Total scores range from 0 to 21.

## How to Read the Score

### Cutoff Ranges

- **0–4:** Minimal anxiety
- **5–9:** Mild
- **10–14:** Moderate
- **15–21:** Severe

A score of 10 is the standard cutoff where further clinical evaluation is recommended. But context matters: a score of 8 during a genuinely stressful week (job change, loss, move) reads differently than an 8 during a calm period. The GAD-7 is best used as a repeated measure, not a one-time verdict.

## Why a Free Online Version Matters

Anxiety is sneaky. It convinces you that what you're feeling is "just how things are" or "not bad enough to bother anyone." A structured screening cuts through that by asking specific, concrete questions — the kind a clinician would ask — and giving you a number you can't rationalize away.

A free, private, no-account version of the GAD-7 is valuable because:

- **Low friction:** You can check in during a lunch break, not a doctor's visit
- **No surveillance:** You don't trade your mental health data for a score
- **Repeatable:** You can take it every two weeks and watch the trend
- **Honest:** The questions are the real clinical items, not a "fun quiz"

## What to Look for in an Online GAD-7

A trustworthy implementation should use the exact validated items, score them correctly, and present the result with appropriate context — not a dramatic "you have severe anxiety!" banner. It should also make clear that the tool screens for symptom severity, not for a diagnosis. And it should never require you to hand over personal information to see your own answers.

## Pairing Screening With Daily Tracking

The GAD-7 captures two weeks at a time. Daily mood tracking captures the texture in between — the bad Monday, the better Thursday, the pattern that emerges over a month. Used together, they give you and any clinician you work with a fuller picture than either alone. A screening tells you the forest; daily logs show you the trees.

## When to Act on the Result

If your score is 10 or above, or if any single item feels acutely true, that's a signal to talk to someone — a primary care doctor, a therapist, a counselor. If you're having thoughts of self-harm, reach out immediately: in the US, call or text 988; text HOME to 741741. The GAD-7 is a screening tool, not a diagnosis — it can't tell you whether you have an anxiety disorder, only how much anxiety has been present lately.
