# Free AUDIT Test Online: What the Alcohol Screening Tool Reveals

Searching for a "free AUDIT test online" often means you've started asking honest questions about your drinking — or someone who cares about you has. The Alcohol Use Disorders Identification Test (AUDIT) is the gold-standard screening tool developed by the World Health Organization to identify hazardous or harmful alcohol use. This article explains what it measures, walks through the structure of its ten questions, breaks down the scoring, and helps you understand what to do with the result.

## What the AUDIT Is

The AUDIT is a 10-item self-report questionnaire developed in 1989 by the WHO as a simple, cross-culturally valid way to screen for problematic alcohol use. Unlike tools that only ask about dependence, the AUDIT covers the full spectrum — from hazardous consumption to dependence symptoms to harmful consequences. It's used in primary care, emergency departments, and mental health settings worldwide.

There's also a shorter version, the AUDIT-C (first three questions only), which screens specifically for hazardous consumption. But the full 10-item version gives a more complete picture.

## The Three Domains

The ten questions break into three domains:

### Domain 1: Hazardous Alcohol Use (items 1–3)
How often you drink, how many drinks on a typical day, and how often you have six or more drinks on one occasion. These three items alone form the AUDIT-C.

### Domain 2: Dependence Symptoms (items 4–6)
Impaired control over drinking, increased salience of drinking (needing a drink first thing in the morning), and morning drinking.

### Domain 3: Harmful Alcohol Use (items 7–10)
Guilt or remorse after drinking, inability to remember what happened, alcohol-related injury, and others suggesting you cut down.

## How Scoring Works

Items 1–8 are scored 0–4; items 9–10 are scored 0, 2, or 4. The total ranges from 0 to 40.

### Standard Cutoffs

- **0–7:** Low risk (for women, some guidelines use 0–6)
- **8–15:** Hazardous or harmful use — brief intervention recommended
- **16–19:** Harmful use — brief intervention plus possibly more intensive treatment
- **20+:** Likely alcohol dependence — further evaluation and referral recommended

A score of 8 or above is the standard threshold indicating a need for further conversation with a clinician.

## Why People Search for a Free Online Version

Alcohol is woven into so many social settings that it's easy to lose track of where "normal" ends and "concerning" begins. A structured screening helps because:

- **It asks specific questions** instead of leaving you to compare yourself to vague norms
- **It's private:** you can answer honestly without fear of judgment
- **It gives you a number** you can bring to a doctor or therapist
- **It separates consumption from consequences:** you might drink less than a friend but still experience harm

A free, no-account, no-ad version is especially important here — the last thing anyone needs is their alcohol screening data being monetized.

## What a Good Online AUDIT Should Do

A respectful implementation should:

- Use the exact WHO-validated items
- Score correctly, including the 0/2/4 scoring for items 9–10
- Show domain-level scores, not just a total
- Avoid moralizing or alarmist language
- Provide resources for cutting down and for seeking help
- Store nothing without explicit consent

## The Honest Framing

The AUDIT doesn't ask whether you're "an alcoholic" — a label many people reject and that isn't clinically precise. It asks whether your drinking pattern carries risk. That's a more useful question. A score of 12 doesn't mean you're broken; it means the way you're drinking is likely causing harm, and it's worth looking at.

Many people who score in the hazardous range aren't dependent. They're drinking in a pattern that's risky — binge episodes, drinking to cope with stress, drinking beyond intended limits — and would benefit from simply cutting down. Brief interventions, sometimes as short as a single structured conversation with a clinician, are surprisingly effective for this group.
