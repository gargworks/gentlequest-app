# Free ISI Test Online: What the Insomnia Severity Index Asks and How to Read It

If you've searched for a "free ISI test online," you're probably looking for a structured way to assess how much your sleep difficulties are affecting you — not just whether you're sleeping poorly, but how severe the impact is. The Insomnia Severity Index (ISI) is the most widely used tool for exactly that. This article walks through what it is, what the questions ask, how to interpret the score, and where a free, private version fits into your routine.

## What the ISI Actually Is

The ISI was developed in 1993 by Charles Morin and colleagues. It's a 7-item self-report questionnaire designed to measure the nature, severity, and impact of insomnia. Unlike sleep diaries that track nightly sleep patterns, the ISI provides a summary measure of insomnia severity over a two-week period.

The ISI is used in clinical settings to assess insomnia severity, monitor treatment response, and screen for clinically significant insomnia. It's also widely used in research. Because it's short, validated, and scored on a simple 0–4 scale, it translates well to a digital format.

## The Seven Items, Summarized

The ISI asks about the past two weeks:

1. **Difficulty falling asleep:** Severity rating (0 = none, 4 = very severe)
2. **Difficulty staying asleep:** Severity rating (0 = none, 4 = very severe)
3. **Problem waking too early:** Severity rating (0 = none, 4 = very severe)
4. **Satisfaction with current sleep pattern:** Rating (0 = very satisfied, 4 = very dissatisfied)
5. **Noticeability of sleep problem to others:** Rating (0 = not at all, 4 = very much)
6. **Worry/distress about sleep:** Rating (0 = not at all, 4 = very much)
7. **Interference with daily functioning:** Rating (0 = not at all, 4 = very much)

The total score ranges from 0 to 28.

## How to Read the Score

### Score Ranges

The conventional severity categories:

- **0–7:** No clinically significant insomnia
- **8–14:** Subthreshold insomnia
- **15–21:** Clinical insomnia (moderate severity)
- **22–28:** Clinical insomnia (severe)

A score of 15 or above is the standard threshold for clinically significant insomnia, warranting further evaluation and potentially treatment. Scores in the subthreshold range (8–14) suggest sleep difficulties that are bothersome but may not meet the threshold for clinical insomnia — though they may still benefit from intervention.

### What the Score Tells You

The ISI measures the severity and impact of insomnia, not just sleep duration. It captures how much your sleep problems bother you, how noticeable they are, and how much they interfere with your daily life. Two people with the same sleep duration can have very different ISI scores depending on the distress and impairment associated with their sleep.

Not every "free ISI test online" is built the same. A respectful implementation should:

- Include the standard "not a diagnosis" framing
- Provide resources for further evaluation if the score is elevated

## Tracking Over Time

The ISI is most useful when taken periodically — every two weeks is the standard interval, matching the recall window. A single score is a snapshot; a trendline is information. If you're tracking sleep anyway, pairing that with a periodic ISI gives you and any clinician you work with a richer picture of whether sleep difficulties are improving, stable, or worsening.

## The Strengths of the ISI

### It Captures Impact, Not Just Duration

The ISI doesn't just ask about sleep — it asks about how sleep problems affect your life. This is important because the clinical significance of insomnia is defined by its consequences (distress, impairment), not just by sleep parameters.

### It's Sensitive to Change

The ISI is well-suited for tracking treatment response. It's commonly used in CBT-I research to measure outcomes, and it's sensitive enough to detect meaningful improvements.

### It's Brief

Seven items is short enough to complete in a few minutes, making it practical for repeated use.

## The Limitations of the ISI

### It's a Screen, Not a Diagnosis

The ISI measures insomnia severity, not diagnostic status. A high score warrants further evaluation by a professional to determine whether you meet criteria for insomnia disorder and what treatment might help.

### It Relies on Self-Report

The ISI asks about your perception of your sleep, not objective sleep measurement. Self-report can be influenced by mood, anxiety about sleep, and other factors. Sleep diaries or actigraphy can provide complementary objective data.

### It Doesn't Assess Other Sleep Disorders

The ISI measures insomnia, not sleep apnea, restless legs syndrome, circadian rhythm disorders, or other sleep conditions. If your sleep problems have a medical cause, the ISI won't identify it.
