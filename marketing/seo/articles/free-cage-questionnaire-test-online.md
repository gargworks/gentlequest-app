# Free CAGE Questionnaire Test Online: What the Alcohol Screening Asks and How to Read It

If you've searched for a "free CAGE questionnaire test online," you're likely looking for a quick, honest way to reflect on your relationship with alcohol — not a diagnosis, not a label, just a structured set of questions that cut through the noise. The CAGE questionnaire is one of the oldest and most widely used screening tools for potential alcohol problems. This article walks through what it is, what the four questions ask, how to interpret the results, and where a free, private version fits into your routine.

## What the CAGE Questionnaire Actually Is

The CAGE was developed in 1987 by Dr. John Ewing. It's a four-question screening tool designed to identify potential alcohol misuse or dependence. The name is an acronym, with each letter representing one of the four questions. It's not a diagnostic instrument — it's a quick screen that helps determine whether further evaluation might be warranted.

Because it's short (four questions), public-domain, and scored on a simple yes/no basis, it translates cleanly to a digital format. That's why "free CAGE questionnaire test online" is a common search: people want a quick, private way to check in with themselves.

## The Four Questions

The CAGE questions are:

1. **C — Cut down:** Have you ever felt you should cut down on your drinking?
2. **A — Annoyed:** Have people annoyed you by criticizing your drinking?
3. **G — Guilty:** Have you ever felt bad or guilty about your drinking?
4. **E — Eye-opener:** Have you ever had a drink first thing in the morning to steady your nerves or to get rid of a hangover (eye-opener)?

Each "yes" answer scores one point. The total ranges from 0 to 4.

## How to Read the Score

### Score Ranges

- **0:** No concerns identified by the screen
- **1:** Possible concern; further exploration may be warranted
- **2 or higher:** Considered a positive screen, suggesting further evaluation is appropriate

A score of 2 or more is the conventional threshold for a "positive" CAGE screen, indicating that a more thorough assessment by a professional may be helpful. However, even a score of 1 — particularly if the "yes" is to the eye-opener question — warrants reflection.

### What the Score Doesn't Tell You

The CAGE is a screen, not a diagnosis. A positive screen doesn't mean you have alcohol use disorder, and a negative screen doesn't necessarily mean everything is fine. The CAGE is designed to detect potential problems, not to assess severity, frequency, or context of drinking. It also doesn't capture current drinking patterns — the questions ask "have you ever," which means a "yes" could reflect a past issue that's since resolved.

## The Limitations of the CAGE

### It's Not Comprehensive

The CAGE doesn't ask about quantity or frequency of drinking — how much, how often, or whether drinking is affecting your health or relationships in ways the four questions don't capture. Other screening tools, like the AUDIT (Alcohol Use Disorders Identification Test), provide a more detailed assessment.

### It Uses Lifetime Framing

The questions ask "have you ever," which means they capture lifetime experience, not current status. Someone who had a drinking problem years ago but has since stopped would still screen positive. This is a limitation for assessing current risk.

### It May Miss Certain Patterns

The CAGE was designed to detect alcohol dependence, not risky drinking patterns that haven't yet led to the experiences the questions describe. Someone who drinks heavily but hasn't yet experienced guilt, criticism, or the need for an eye-opener would screen negative.

## Tracking Over Time

The CAGE is most useful as a one-time or occasional screen, not a frequent tracker. Because of its lifetime framing, retaking it frequently doesn't provide new information. If you're looking for ongoing monitoring, the AUDIT or a simple drinking diary may be more useful.
