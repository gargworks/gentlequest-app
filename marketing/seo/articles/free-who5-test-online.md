# Free WHO-5 Test Online: What the Well-Being Index Asks and How to Read It

If you've searched for a "free WHO-5 test online," you're probably looking for a quick way to check in on your overall well-being — not just whether you're struggling, but whether you're genuinely thriving. The WHO-5 Well-Being Index is one of the most widely used tools for measuring positive well-being. This article walks through what it is, what the five questions ask, how to interpret the score, and where a free, private version fits into your routine.

## What the WHO-5 Actually Is

The WHO-5 was developed in 1998 by the World Health Organization's Regional Office for Europe. Unlike most mental health screening tools, which focus on symptoms and distress, the WHO-5 measures positive well-being — the presence of good feelings, energy, and engagement, not just the absence of bad ones.

This positive framing is important. It means the WHO-5 can detect when someone is languishing (functioning poorly without meeting criteria for a mental disorder) even when symptom-based screens come back normal. It's used in clinical settings, research, and general population health surveys worldwide.

## The Five Questions

Over the last two weeks, how often have you:

1. Felt cheerful and in good spirits
2. Felt calm and relaxed
3. Felt active and vigorous
4. Woken up feeling fresh and rested
5. Felt that your daily life has been filled with things that interest you

Each answer is scored on a 6-point scale: all of the time = 5, most of the time = 4, more than half of the time = 3, less than half of the time = 2, some of the time = 1, at no time = 0. The total is calculated by summing the five items and multiplying by 4, giving a range from 0 (worst imaginable well-being) to 100 (best imaginable well-being).

## How to Read the Score

### Score Ranges

- **0–50:** Poor well-being — further screening for depression is recommended
- **51–100:** Good well-being

A score of 50 or below (raw score of 13 or below on the 0–25 scale before multiplication) is the standard threshold for screening for depression. At this level, the WHO-5 recommends further assessment using a depression-specific tool like the PHQ-9.

### What the Score Tells You

The WHO-5 measures the presence of positive well-being, not the absence of symptoms. A low score doesn't mean you're depressed — it means you're not experiencing adequate well-being, which may or may not be related to a mental health condition. A high score doesn't mean you have no mental health concerns — it means your positive well-being is currently good.

## Tracking Over Time

The WHO-5 is most useful when taken periodically — every two weeks is the standard interval, matching the recall window. A single score is a snapshot; a trendline is information. Tracking well-being over time can reveal patterns that a one-time check won't: seasonal changes, the impact of life events, or the effects of treatment.

## The Strengths of the WHO-5

### It Measures the Positive

Most mental health screens ask about what's wrong. The WHO-5 asks about what's right. This matters because mental health isn't just the absence of illness — it's the presence of well-being. The WHO-5 captures this dimension.

### It's Brief

Five questions is extremely short, making it practical for frequent use. Despite its brevity, it performs well as a screen for depression and as a measure of general well-being.

### It's Internationally Validated

The WHO-5 has been translated into more than 30 languages and validated across diverse populations. Its cutoffs are well-established internationally.

## The Limitations of the WHO-5

### It's Not a Diagnostic Tool

The WHO-5 measures well-being, not disorders. A low score warrants further evaluation, not a diagnosis.

### It Doesn't Assess Specific Symptoms

The WHO-5 doesn't ask about specific depression or anxiety symptoms. If your score is low, a follow-up with a more specific tool (like the PHQ-9 or GAD-7) is recommended.

### It Doesn't Assess Risk

The WHO-5 doesn't include items about self-harm or suicide. If you're experiencing thoughts of self-harm, the WHO-5 won't flag this — and you should seek help regardless of your score.
