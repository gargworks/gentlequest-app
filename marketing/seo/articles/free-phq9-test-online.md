# Free PHQ-9 Test Online: What the Depression Screening Asks and How to Read It

If you've searched for a "free PHQ-9 test online," you're probably looking for a quick, honest way to check in with yourself — not a diagnosis, not a label, just a structured snapshot of how the last two weeks have felt. The Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9) is one of the most widely used screening tools for that exact purpose. This article walks through what it is, what the nine questions actually ask, how to interpret the score ranges, and where a free, private version fits into your routine.

## What the PHQ-9 Actually Is

The PHQ-9 is a nine-item self-report questionnaire developed in the late 1990s as a shorter alternative to longer clinician-administered interviews. Each item maps to one of the core criteria for major depressive episode in the DSM. It's not a personality test, a mood quiz, or a wellness score — it's a structured screening instrument that primary care doctors, therapists, and researchers use to gauge symptom severity and track change over time.

Because it's short, public-domain, and scored on a simple 0–3 scale, it translates cleanly to a digital format. That's why "free PHQ-9 test online" is such a common search: people want the same tool their doctor would use, without the waiting room.

## The Nine Questions, Summarized

Over the last two weeks, how often have you been bothered by:

1. Little interest or pleasure in doing things
2. Feeling down, depressed, or hopeless
3. Trouble falling/staying asleep, or sleeping too much
4. Feeling tired or having little energy
5. Poor appetite or overeating
6. Feeling bad about yourself — or that you're a failure or have let people down
7. Trouble concentrating on things like reading or watching television
8. Moving or speaking so slowly that other people noticed, or being so fidgety that you've been moving around a lot more than usual
9. Thoughts that you'd be better off dead, or of hurting yourself in some way

Each answer is scored: not at all = 0, several days = 1, more than half the days = 2, nearly every day = 3. The ninth question is a safety item — any response above zero warrants talking to a professional immediately.

## How to Read the Score

The total ranges from 0 to 27. The conventional cutoffs:

### Score Ranges

- **0–4:** Minimal or none
- **5–9:** Mild
- **10–14:** Moderate
- **15–19:** Moderately severe
- **20–27:** Severe

A score of 10 or above is the usual threshold where clinicians consider further evaluation. But a number alone never tells the whole story — item-level detail matters more than the total. Someone with a 9 who answered "nearly every day" to question 9 needs urgent support, regardless of the overall score being "mild."

## What a Good Online PHQ-9 Should Do

Not every "free PHQ-9 test online" is built the same. A respectful implementation should:

- Show the exact nine items without paraphrasing
- Score transparently, showing which items contributed
- Flag question 9 clearly with crisis resources
- Not store your answers without explicit consent
- Not require an account to see results
- Include the standard "not a diagnosis" framing

## Tracking Over Time

The PHQ-9 is most useful when taken periodically — every two weeks is the standard interval, matching the recall window. A single score is a snapshot; a trendline is information. If you're logging mood anyway, pairing that with a periodic screening gives you and any clinician you work with a richer picture.

## The Important Caveat

The PHQ-9 is a screening tool, not a diagnostic instrument. A high score doesn't mean you have major depressive disorder, and a low score doesn't mean you're fine. It's a prompt for conversation — with yourself, and if the score or any individual item concerns you, with a qualified mental health professional.
