# Safety Plan App: A Calm Tool for Your Hardest Moments

A safety plan is a personal, written plan for getting through a crisis — the steps you'll take, the people you'll call, the things that have helped before. It's a recognized tool in mental health care, and having it on your phone means it's there when you need it most. If you're looking for a **safety plan app**, here's what to look for and how to use one.

## What a Safety Plan Is

### A Personal Plan for Hard Moments

A safety plan is something you write when you're calm, to use when you're not. It lists the warning signs that you're heading into crisis, the coping strategies that have helped before, the people and places that provide distraction or support, and the professionals and crisis lines to contact if things escalate.

### Not a Replacement for Treatment

A safety plan is a complement to professional care, not a substitute. It's a tool that sits between you and the moment of crisis — a bridge to the help you need.

## What a Safety Plan App Should Offer

### The Standard Safety Plan Structure

A good app follows the evidence-based structure:

1. **Warning signs** — thoughts, feelings, or situations that signal you're heading into crisis
2. **Coping strategies** — things you can do on your own to reduce the intensity
3. **People and places** — who or where provides distraction and calm
4. **People to ask for help** — specific names and numbers
5. **Professionals and crisis lines** — therapists, hotlines, emergency services

### Easy to Fill Out When Calm

The app should make it simple to write your plan when you're in a good place. Clear prompts, no jargon, no pressure.

### One-Tap Access in Crisis

When you're in a hard moment, you shouldn't have to navigate menus. The plan should be reachable in one tap, with phone numbers and crisis lines clickable.

### Private and Local

Your safety plan is deeply personal. The app should store it locally, not share it, and not require an account that syncs your most vulnerable information to a server.

## How to Build a Safety Plan

### Do It When You're Steady

Write your plan when you're calm and thinking clearly. Don't wait for a crisis to start — that's the worst time to think clearly.

### Be Specific

Instead of "call a friend," write "call [name] at [number]." Instead of "do something calming," write "walk outside for 10 minutes" or "listen to [specific playlist]." Specific is memorable and actionable.

### Include Crisis Lines

Add the crisis lines relevant to your country or region. If you're not sure which ones to include, a quick search now — while you're calm — will give you the numbers to save.

### Review and Update

Your plan isn't set in stone. Review it every few months, especially after a hard period, and update it with what you've learned.

## What a Safety Plan App Should Not Do

### No Ads in a Crisis Tool

An ad in a safety plan app is not just annoying — it's dangerous. In a crisis moment, an interruption can derail the steps you're trying to follow. An ad-free app is essential.

### No Streaks or Gamification

A safety plan isn't a habit to maintain. It's a tool for the worst moments. Gamification has no place here.

### No AI Analysis

Your safety plan should not be scanned, summarized, or "insight-ed" by an algorithm. It's yours, and it should stay yours.

## A Note on What This Isn't

This article is for general information only. It's not a diagnosis, and it doesn't replace professional care. A safety plan is one tool in a broader support system. If you're struggling or in crisis, please reach out to a qualified mental health professional, a crisis line, or emergency services immediately. You don't have to handle this alone.
