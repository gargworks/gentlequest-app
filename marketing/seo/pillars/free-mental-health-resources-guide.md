# The Comprehensive Free Mental Health Resources Guide: No-Cost Tools for Wellbeing

Mental health support shouldn't require a credit card. Yet the mental health landscape is increasingly monetized — subscription apps, paywalled content, and "freemium" models that gate essential tools behind monthly fees. This guide is a comprehensive resource for genuinely free mental health tools: screening tests, coping techniques, educational materials, and apps that don't charge for core features.

## Why Free Matters

### The Access Problem

Mental health care is expensive. Therapy costs $100-300 per session. Psychiatrist visits cost even more. Insurance coverage is inconsistent, and many people are uninsured or underinsured. Free resources don't replace professional care, but they provide a baseline of support for people who can't access or afford more.

### The Principle

Mental health tools — especially basic ones like screening tests and coping techniques — should be free because:

- **They're based on public-domain instruments:** The GAD-7, PHQ-9, and other screening tools were developed with public funding and are free to use.
- **The techniques are ancient and universal:** Breathing, grounding, and journaling don't belong to anyone.
- **Charging for basics exploits vulnerability:** People seeking basic mental health support are often in distress; paywalling that support is exploitative.
- **Free tools reduce barriers:** The easier it is to access support, the more people will use it.

## Free Screening Tests

Screening tests are validated questionnaires that measure the severity of mental health symptoms. They're not diagnoses — they're tools for self-awareness and tracking. All of the following are public-domain instruments that should be available for free, with no account required and no data harvesting.

### Anxiety Screening

**GAD-7 (Generalized Anxiety Disorder-7):** The most common anxiety screening tool. Seven questions, two minutes, scores from 0-21. Our [free GAD-7 test guide](/articles/free-gad7-test-online) covers what it measures and how to interpret the results.

### Depression Screening

**PHQ-9 (Patient Health Questionnaire-9):** The standard depression screening tool. Nine questions, two minutes, scores from 0-27. Our [free PHQ-9 test guide](/articles/free-phq9-test-online) covers what it measures and how to read the score.

### Multi-Domain Screening

**DASS-21 (Depression Anxiety Stress Scale-21):** Measures all three domains in one 21-item questionnaire. Our [free DASS-21 test guide](/articles/free-dass-21-test-online) covers how it works.

**K10 (Kessler Psychological Distress Scale):** A 10-item measure of general psychological distress. Our [free K10 test guide](/articles/free-k10-test-online) covers what it asks and how to read it.

**WHO-5 (Well-Being Index):** Measures positive well-being, not just the absence of symptoms. Our [free WHO-5 test guide](/articles/free-who5-test-online) covers this unique instrument.

### Sleep Screening

**ISI (Insomnia Severity Index):** Screens for insomnia severity. Our [free ISI test guide](/articles/free-isi-test-online) covers what it asks.

**SSS (Stanford Sleepiness Scale):** Measures daytime sleepiness. Our [free SSS test guide](/articles/free-sss-test-online) covers this quick tool.

### Stress Screening

**PSS (Perceived Stress Scale):** Measures how stressful you perceive your life to be. Our [free PSS test guide](/articles/free-pss-test-online) covers what it reveals.

### Trauma Screening

**PCL-5 (PTSD Checklist for DSM-5):** Screens for PTSD symptoms. Our [free PCL-5 test guide](/articles/free-pcl5-test-online) covers this tool.

**ACE (Adverse Childhood Experiences):** Measures childhood trauma exposure. Our [free ACE test guide](/articles/free-ace-test-online) covers what it reveals.

### Substance Use Screening

**AUDIT (Alcohol Use Disorders Identification Test):** Screens for problematic alcohol use. Our [free AUDIT test guide](/articles/free-audit-test-online) covers this tool.

**CAGE Questionnaire:** A brief alcohol screening tool. Our [free CAGE questionnaire test guide](/articles/free-cage-questionnaire-test-online) covers what it asks.

### How to Use Screening Tests

1. **Take them regularly:** Every two weeks for symptom tracking; monthly for general monitoring.
2. **Track the trend:** Individual scores matter less than the direction over time.
3. **Don't diagnose yourself:** Screening tools indicate severity, not diagnosis. Only a professional can diagnose.
4. **Share with professionals:** If you're seeing a therapist or doctor, share your screening results.
5. **Use them as a signal:** A score that's increasing over time is a signal to seek help or adjust your approach.

## Free Coping Techniques

These techniques require no equipment, no subscription, and no app. They're the most accessible mental health tools available.

### Breathing Techniques

**Box breathing (4-4-4-4):** Inhale for 4, hold for 4, exhale for 4, hold for 4. Our [box breathing guide](/articles/box-breathing-app) covers this technique.

**Extended exhale breathing:** Inhale for 4, exhale for 6-8. Our [breathing exercise guide](/articles/breathing-exercise-app-free) covers this in depth.

**4-7-8 breathing:** Inhale for 4, hold for 7, exhale for 8. Particularly useful for sleep.

**Physiological sigh:** Double inhale through the nose, long exhale through the mouth. Rapid anxiety reduction.

### Grounding Techniques

**5-4-3-2-1:** Name 5 things you see, 4 you hear, 3 you touch, 2 you smell, 1 you taste. Our [5-4-3-2-1 grounding guide](/articles/5-4-3-2-1-grounding-app) covers this step by step.

**Physical grounding:** Press feet into the floor, hold something cold, notice your body's weight. Our [grounding exercise guide](/articles/grounding-exercise-app) covers additional techniques.

**Cognitive grounding:** Count backward from 100 by 7s, name categories, describe your environment.

### Progressive Muscle Relaxation

Systematically tense and release each muscle group from feet to head. Our [progressive muscle relaxation guide](/articles/progressive-muscle-relaxation-app) provides a full walkthrough.

### CBT Skills

**Thought records:** Write down the situation, thought, emotion, evidence for, evidence against, alternative thought, and new emotion. Our [CBT thought record guide](/articles/cbt-thought-record-app) and [thought record guide](/articles/thought-record-app) cover this practice.

**Cognitive restructuring:** Identify and challenge cognitive distortions (catastrophizing, all-or-nothing thinking, mind-reading). Our [free CBT guide](/articles/cbt-app-free) provides a structured introduction.

**Behavioral experiments:** Test your beliefs against reality. Our [CBT behavioral experiment guide](/articles/cbt-behavioral-experiment-app) explains how.

### Behavioral Activation

Schedule small, valued activities and do them regardless of mood. Our [behavioral activation guide](/articles/behavioral-activation-app) covers this approach.

### Journaling

Write about your thoughts, feelings, and experiences. Our [journaling guide](/articles/journal-app-no-ads) covers different journaling methods for mental health.

### Self-Compassion

Treat yourself with the kindness you'd offer a friend. Our [self-compassion guide](/articles/self-compassion-app) explores this practice.

### Mindfulness

Practice non-judgmental present-moment awareness. Our [mindfulness guide](/articles/mindfulness-app-no-ads) covers mindfulness practices.

## Free Apps

### What "Free" Should Mean

A genuinely free mental health app should offer its core features at no cost, with no hidden charges, no data selling, and no ad-supported model. The core features that should always be free include:

- Screening tools (GAD-7, PHQ-9, etc.)
- Breathing exercises
- Grounding techniques
- Basic mood tracking
- Safety planning

### The Free App Checklist

When evaluating a "free" mental health app, check:

1. **Are the core tools actually free?** Or is the "free" version just a teaser?
2. **Is it ad-supported?** Ads in a mental health app are harmful. Our [free mental health app with no ads guide](/articles/free-mental-health-app-no-ads) covers this.
3. **Does it sell your data?** Check the privacy policy.
4. **Does it require a subscription for essential features?** Screening tools and basic coping techniques should never be paywalled.
5. **Does it use dark patterns?** Streaks, guilt-tripping notifications, and fake urgency are red flags. Our [mental health app with no streaks guide](/articles/mental-health-app-no-streaks) covers this.

## Free Crisis Resources

If you're in crisis, these resources are free, confidential, and available 24/7:

- **988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline:** Call or text 988 (US)
- **Crisis Text Line:** Text HOME to 741741 (US)
- **Veterans Crisis Line:** Call 988, then press 1, or text 838255
- **The Trevor Project:** Call 1-866-488-7386 or text START to 678678 (LGBTQ+ youth)
- **National Domestic Violence Hotline:** Call 1-800-799-7233 or text LOVEIS to 22522

If you're outside the US, the International Association for Suicide Prevention (IASP) maintains a directory of crisis centers worldwide.

## When Free Isn't Enough

Free resources are valuable, but they're not a substitute for professional care when it's needed. If you're experiencing:

- Moderate to severe symptoms
- Symptoms that aren't improving with self-help
- Thoughts of self-harm
- Significant impairment in daily functioning

...please seek professional help. Many areas offer sliding-scale therapy, community mental health centers, and other low-cost options. A [therapy alternative app](/articles/therapy-alternative-app) can bridge gaps, but it's not a replacement for professional treatment.

If you're having thoughts of self-harm, reach out immediately: in the US, call or text 988, or text HOME to 741741.

## The Ethics of Free Mental Health Resources

### Why Basic Tools Should Be Free

The argument for free basic mental health tools is both practical and ethical:

- **Practical:** Free tools reduce barriers, increase access, and reach people who would otherwise go unsupported.
- **Ethical:** Mental health is a fundamental aspect of human wellbeing. Basic tools for managing it — screening, breathing, grounding — should not be gated by ability to pay.
- **Public health:** Wider access to basic tools reduces the burden on the healthcare system by helping people manage mild to moderate symptoms before they become severe.

### When Charging Is Appropriate

Charging for mental health products is appropriate when:

- The product provides significant value beyond the basics (e.g., personalized therapy, specialized programs)
- The pricing is transparent and fair
- A meaningful free tier is available
- The product doesn't exploit vulnerability (e.g., no paywalls during crisis moments)

### The Problem With "Freemium" Mental Health Apps

Many "freemium" mental health apps offer a limited free tier and gate essential features behind a subscription. The problems:

- The free tier is often so limited it's useless
- The subscription is often expensive ($60-100/year)
- The paywall often appears when the user is most vulnerable
- The free tier may still harvest data, meaning you pay with your privacy even when you don't pay with money

A genuinely free tier should include the core tools — screening, breathing, grounding, basic tracking — without data harvesting or ad support.
