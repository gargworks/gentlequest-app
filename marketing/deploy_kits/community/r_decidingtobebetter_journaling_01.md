## Post
- **Title:** I finally stuck with journaling after failing 10+ times — here's what changed
- **Body:**

I've started journaling probably a dozen times in my life and quit within a week every time. The reasons varied: too tired, nothing to say, forgot, felt forced, the app annoyed me, I missed a day and felt behind.

What finally made it stick was dropping every assumption I had about what journaling "should" be:

- No minimum length. One sentence is fine. One word is fine.
- No daily requirement. I write when I have something to say, not on a schedule.
- No prompts I have to answer. Sometimes I just vent. Sometimes I write a list. Sometimes it's a single complaint.
- No re-reading obligation. It's not a diary I'll treasure. It's a place for thoughts to go so they stop circling.

The key insight for me: journaling isn't about creating a record. It's about getting thoughts out of my head so my brain stops treating them as unresolved. The page is a disposal system, not a museum.

I'm about two months in now and it's the longest I've ever kept it going. Not because I'm disciplined now but because I made it easy enough that quitting would take more effort than continuing.
