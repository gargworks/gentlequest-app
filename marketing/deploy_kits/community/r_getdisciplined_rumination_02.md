## Post
- **Title:** How I deal with rumination without spiraling — the 'write it and close it' method
- **Body:**

I'm a chronic ruminator. Something goes wrong or feels unresolved and my brain will chew on it for hours, days, sometimes weeks. It feels productive — like I'm "working through it" — but it's actually just spinning.

The technique that's helped most: when I notice I'm ruminating, I write the whole thought spiral down in one sitting. Everything. The what-if chain, the regret, the self-criticism, all of it. Then I physically close it and say out loud "that's filed."

The act of externalizing it does something. In my head, the thoughts feel urgent and infinite. On a page, they're finite — usually the same 4-5 sentences repeated in different configurations. Seeing that makes it harder to take the spiral seriously.

I don't re-read it immediately. Sometimes I come back days later and the thing I was agonizing over looks small or even incomprehensible. That builds a kind of evidence base that my rumination isn't as wise as it feels in the moment.

The discipline part is doing this *every time* instead of just letting it run. It takes practice. But it's been more effective than any "just stop thinking about it" advice I've ever gotten.
