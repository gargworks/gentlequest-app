## Post
- **Title:** Perfectionism is killing my discipline — here's what's actually working
- **Body:**

I've read all the productivity advice. Systems, frameworks, time-blocking, atomic habits. The problem was never not knowing what to do — it was that perfectionism made starting feel pointless unless I could do it perfectly.

If I couldn't do the full workout, I wouldn't do any. If I couldn't write the complete essay, I wouldn't write a paragraph. If I couldn't meditate for 20 minutes, I wouldn't sit at all. All-or-nothing, every time.

The thing that's slowly breaking this pattern: writing down the "imperfect version" of what I plan to do, deliberately, before I start. Not "workout" but "10 push-ups, even if I stop there." Not "write 2000 words" but "open the doc and write one sentence." Then logging what I actually did without editorializing about whether it was enough.

The shift is from "did I do it perfectly?" to "did I do something?" It sounds trivial but for me it's been the difference between doing nothing most days and doing something most days. The imperfect thing compounds. The perfect thing never starts.

Anyone else struggle with this? What helped you lower the bar enough to actually clear it?
