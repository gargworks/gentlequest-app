## Post
- **Title:** Behavioral activation: the unsexy CBT technique that actually works when you're flattened
- **Body:**

When people think CBT they usually think cognitive restructuring — challenging thoughts, thought records, reframing. But behavioral activation is the other half and in my experience it's more effective when you're too depressed to do the cognitive work.

The principle: mood follows behavior, not just the other way around. You don't wait to feel better to do things — you do small things and feeling better follows, gradually. It sounds wrong when you're depressed ("I can't even shower, how am I supposed to go for a walk?") but that's why the activities have to be genuinely small.

My therapist had me rate activities by difficulty on a 1-10 scale and start at the 1s. Not "go to the gym" (8) but "walk to the mailbox" (1). Not "cook a meal" (6) but "boil water for tea" (1).

The CBT part is tracking what you did and how you felt before and after. Almost every time, my mood after was better than my predicted mood before. Over weeks, that data chips away at the "nothing will help" belief.

It's not instant. It's not dramatic. But it's one of the most evidence-based CBT components and it doesn't get talked about enough.
