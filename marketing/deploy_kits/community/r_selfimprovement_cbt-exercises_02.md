## Post
- **Title:** CBT thought records changed how I think — but they're tedious unless you make them easy
- **Body:**

A therapist taught me thought records years ago and they're genuinely one of the most useful tools I've encountered. The basic idea: when you're spiraling, you write down the thought, the evidence for it, the evidence against it, and a more balanced alternative.

It sounds like homework because it is. And that's why I kept quitting. When you're in a negative spiral, the last thing you want to do is fill out a 6-column worksheet. The friction kills it.

What made it stick: having the format ready to go on my phone, so when I notice I'm spiraling I can open it and start typing immediately. No finding a notebook, no remembering the columns, no setting up. The structure is already there, I just fill in the blanks.

The other thing that helped: not being a perfectionist about it. Sometimes I can't find "evidence against" and that's fine — just writing the thought down and sitting with the "evidence for" column often reveals how thin the case actually is.

I don't do it every day. I do it when I notice the spiral. And that's been enough to slowly change how I relate to my own thoughts — not believing everything I think just because I thought it.
