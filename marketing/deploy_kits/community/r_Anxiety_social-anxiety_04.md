## Post
- **Title:** Social anxiety: I started writing down what I was actually afraid would happen
- **Body:**

Something that's been slowly helping with my social anxiety. Before situations that normally trigger me — meetings, group dinners, phone calls — I've started writing down the specific fear. Not "I'm anxious" but the actual predicted catastrophe.

"I'm going to say something stupid and everyone will think I'm incompetent."
"They'll notice I'm nervous and it'll be awkward."
"I won't have anything to say and there'll be silence."

Then afterward I go back and note what actually happened. Almost every time, the predicted disaster didn't occur — or if something awkward happened, it was minor and nobody cared the way I imagined.

It sounds simple to the point of being dumb, but I'd never actually articulated the specific fears. They were just this roiling cloud of dread. Writing them down made them concrete and, honestly, a little ridiculous in the light of day.

Has anyone else done prediction testing like this? Did it stick for you or did you stop doing it after a while?
