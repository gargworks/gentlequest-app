## Post
- **Title:** My therapist had me create a safety plan and I didn't take it seriously until I needed it
- **Body:**

A while back my therapist suggested we make a safety plan. I agreed but internally I thought it was a formality — something we had to check off. I wasn't in crisis, I felt okay at the time, and filling out a form about "warning signs" and "coping strategies" felt slightly dramatic.

Then a few months later I hit a really bad stretch. And in that stretch, my brain was absolutely not capable of remembering what helps, who to call, or why I should stay. Everything I'd discussed with my therapist was just... gone. Accessible in theory, inaccessible in practice.

But the safety plan was on my phone. I opened it and there it was — my own handwriting (well, typing) telling me what to do, in my own voice, based on what past-me knew about what helps. I followed it. It got me through the night.

I share this because if your therapist suggests a safety plan and you think "I don't really need that" — make it anyway. You make it when you're well because you can't make it when you're not. That's the whole point.
