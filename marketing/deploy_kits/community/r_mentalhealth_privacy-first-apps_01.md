## Post
- **Title:** Why I switched to a privacy-first mental health app (and what that actually means)
- **Body:**

I used a popular mental health app for a while and eventually got uncomfortable with how much of my inner life was sitting on someone's server. Journal entries about my worst moments, mood data, patterns — all synced to a company that could change their privacy policy, get acquired, or get breached.

I started looking into what "privacy-first" actually means for these apps and it's a bit of a buzzword. Some apps say "we respect your privacy" but still sync everything to their servers. The version I actually wanted was: my entries never leave my device unless I explicitly export them.

I found one that does this — entries are local, no account required, no cloud. It's been a relief honestly. I write more honestly knowing it's not going to be part of some dataset or show up in an AI-generated "weekly insight" email.

Not trying to fearmonger — most companies probably do handle data responsibly. But for something as personal as mental health journaling, I'd rather not have to trust them at all.

What do you all look for in mental health apps privacy-wise?
