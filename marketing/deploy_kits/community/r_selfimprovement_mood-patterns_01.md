## Post
- **Title:** I tracked my mood for 60 days and found patterns I never would've guessed
- **Body:**

I've always assumed my bad days were random — just part of who I am. But I tracked my mood casually for about two months (no rigid system, just quick notes when I felt like it) and the patterns were not what I expected.

Things I found:
- My mood dips hard on Sundays. Not Monday dread — Sunday itself. The "weekend winding down" feeling hits me harder than the actual workweek.
- I feel best in the 2 hours after exercising, even if it's just a walk. I knew exercise helped but I didn't realize the window was that immediate.
- Bad sleep doesn't tank my mood the next day — it tanks it the day *after*. A one-day lag I never would've noticed without tracking.
- Social contact matters more than I thought. Days with even a brief non-work conversation scored noticeably higher.

None of these are universal truths — they're just *my* patterns. But that's the point. Generic advice ("get sunlight, exercise, sleep well") only helps if you know which levers actually move the needle for you specifically.

Has anyone else done this and found surprising patterns? I'm curious how common the Sunday dip is.
