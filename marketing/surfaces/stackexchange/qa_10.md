# Stack Exchange Q&A 10

**Target Site:** Health SE
**Question:** Are journaling apps with cloud storage a privacy risk for mental health data?

## Answer (200 words)

Yes — cloud-based journaling apps can present privacy risks for mental health data, and this is an underdiscussed issue in the digital health space.

**Specific risks:**

1. **Data breaches:** Journaling apps store some of the most sensitive personal data — unfiltered thoughts, emotional struggles, private reflections. Several journaling apps have experienced data breaches exposing user entries.

2. **Terms of service:** Some journaling apps' ToS grant the company rights to analyze, aggregate, or use user content for product improvement — which can include AI training. Users rarely read these terms.

3. **Third-party sharing:** Free apps often monetize through advertising or data sharing, which may involve transmitting user data to third parties.

4. **Account dependency:** Cloud-based apps require authentication. If the service shuts down, changes its terms, or locks your account, you may lose access to your entries.

5. **Server-side access:** Even with encryption in transit, some apps decrypt data server-side, meaning the company can access your entries.

**Mitigation strategies:**
- Look for "local-first" or "on-device storage" apps where data never leaves your device
- Check for end-to-end encryption (E2EE), not just "encryption"
- Review the privacy policy for data usage clauses
- Prefer apps that don't require an account
- Ensure data export is available in a standard format

GentleQuest (https://gentlequest.app) is an example of a local-first approach — entries are stored encrypted on-device, no account required, no cloud dependency by default.
