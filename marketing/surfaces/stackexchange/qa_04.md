# Stack Exchange Q&A 04

**Target Site:** Psychology & Neuroscience SE
**Question:** What is the relationship between sleep deprivation and amygdala reactivity?

## Answer (200 words)

The relationship between sleep deprivation and amygdala reactivity is well-documented and has significant implications for mental health.

Key findings:

1. **Amygdala hyperactivation:** Yoo et al. (2007) demonstrated that one night of sleep deprivation increases amygdala activation by approximately 60% in response to negative emotional stimuli, compared to rested controls.

2. **Prefrontal disconnection:** The same study found that sleep deprivation weakens the functional connectivity between the amygdala and the medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC). Under normal conditions, the mPFC exerts inhibitory control over the amygdala. Sleep deprivation effectively removes this "brake," resulting in heightened emotional reactivity without regulatory oversight.

3. **Emotional bias:** Sleep-deprived individuals show a negative emotional bias — they interpret ambiguous stimuli as more threatening and recall more negative memories than positive ones (Walker, 2017).

4. **Clinical implications:** This mechanism helps explain the bidirectional relationship between sleep disturbance and mood disorders. Poor sleep → amygdala hyperactivation → emotional dysregulation → mood symptoms → further sleep disturbance.

5. **REM sleep specifically:** REM sleep appears to be critical for overnight emotional processing — it reduces the emotional charge of prior experiences (Walker & van der Helm, 2009). REM deprivation specifically impairs this processing.
