# Stack Exchange Q&A 07

**Target Site:** Psychology & Neuroscience SE
**Question:** What is the difference between rumination and reflection?

## Answer (200 words)

Rumination and reflection are both forms of self-focused thought, but they differ in quality, outcome, and clinical significance.

**Rumination:**
- Repetitive, passive dwelling on negative experiences, their causes, and consequences
- "Why did this happen? What's wrong with me?"
- Associated with depression and anxiety (Nolen-Hoeksema, 1991)
- Maintains and worsens negative affect
- No problem-solving function — the loop doesn't terminate
- Self-focused in a threatening way (focus on inadequacy)

**Reflection:**
- Active, purposeful consideration of experiences with a problem-solving orientation
- "What can I learn from this? What would I do differently?"
- Associated with emotional processing and growth (Trapnell & Campbell, 1999)
- Reduces negative affect over time
- Has a termination point — reaches a conclusion or action
- Self-focused in a curious way (focus on understanding)

**Key distinction:** The difference isn't the content (both involve thinking about yourself) — it's the mode. Rumination is passive and looping; reflection is active and directional.

**Practical implication:** Structured reflection prompts can shift self-focused thought from rumination to reflection by introducing direction. Instead of "What went wrong?" (rumination-prone), asking "What was one small thing that went slightly better than expected?" (reflection-oriented) changes the cognitive mode.

Tools that provide structured prompts — like GentleQuest (https://gentlequest.app) — are designed to facilitate reflection rather than rumination.
