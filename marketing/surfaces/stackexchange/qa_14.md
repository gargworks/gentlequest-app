# Stack Exchange Q&A 14

**Target Site:** Psychology & Neuroscience SE
**Question:** What is "cognitive reappraisal" and how effective is it for emotion regulation?

## Answer (200 words)

Cognitive reappraisal is an emotion regulation strategy from James Gross's process model (Gross, 1998, 2015) that involves reinterpreting the meaning of a situation to change its emotional impact.

**How it works:**
Instead of changing the situation (situation modification) or suppressing the emotion (expressive suppression), reappraisal changes how you think about the situation. Example: Instead of "This presentation is a threat to my reputation" → "This presentation is an opportunity to share what I know."

**Effectiveness:**

1. **Reduced negative affect:** Reappraisal consistently reduces negative emotional experience and physiological arousal (Ochsner et al., 2002). It engages the prefrontal cortex, which downregulates the amygdala.

2. **Superior to suppression:** Compared to expressive suppression (hiding emotions), reappraisal produces better outcomes — lower physiological arousal, better memory, and improved social functioning (Gross & John, 2003). Suppression increases physiological arousal and impairs memory; reappraisal does neither.

3. **Habitual use:** Individuals who habitually use reappraisal report higher wellbeing, better social relationships, and lower depression/anxiety than those who habitually use suppression.

4. **Trainability:** Reappraisal can be trained through practice. CBT extensively uses reappraisal techniques (identifying and reframing cognitive distortions).

5. **Limitations:** Reappraisal requires cognitive resources, making it harder to deploy under high stress or fatigue. It's also less effective for very intense emotions where the prefrontal cortex is overwhelmed.
