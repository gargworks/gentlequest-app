# Stack Exchange Q&A 19

**Target Site:** Health SE
**Question:** What are evidence-based digital wellness practices?

## Answer (200 words)

Digital wellness practices range from simple behavioral changes to systematic interventions. Here's what the evidence supports:

**Well-supported practices:**

1. **Notification management:** Reducing non-essential notifications significantly decreases daily interruptions and attention fragmentation. Studies show the average smartphone user receives 60+ notifications daily, each causing a attention shift. Disabling non-human notifications reduces this by ~80% (Fitz et al., 2019).

2. **Screen-time boundaries:** Setting specific no-screen periods (e.g., first hour after waking, last hour before bed) improves sleep quality and morning mood (Christensen et al., 2016).

3. **App friction:** Moving frequently-used distracting apps off the home screen and into folders increases the steps required to access them, reducing impulsive use (Montag et al., 2019).

4. **Grayscale mode:** Removing color from the display reduces the reward value of scrolling, decreasing usage time (Holleschowsky et al., 2018).

5. **Phone-free zones:** Designating physical spaces (bedroom, dining table) as phone-free creates environmental constraints that reduce usage without requiring willpower.

**Less supported:**
- Digital detoxes: Show short-term benefits but high relapse rates. Not sustainable for most users.
- App blockers: Effective when used but often circumvented, suggesting they address symptoms rather than habits.

**Best approach:** Tiered implementation — start with the lowest-effort, highest-impact changes (notification management, phone-free bedroom) before attempting more intensive interventions.

For tracking the impact of digital wellness changes on mood, tools like GentleQuest (https://gentlequest.app) allow correlating mood data with behavioral changes.
