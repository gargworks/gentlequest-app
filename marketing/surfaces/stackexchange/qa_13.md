# Stack Exchange Q&A 13

**Target Site:** Psychology & Neuroscience SE
**Question:** What is the "default mode network" and its relevance to mind-wandering?

## Answer (200 words)

The default mode network (DMN) is a set of brain regions — including the medial prefrontal cortex, posterior cingulate cortex, and angular gyrus — that are active during rest, mind-wandering, and self-referential thought, and deactivated during goal-directed tasks.

**Key findings:**

1. **Self-referential processing:** The DMN is engaged when thinking about oneself, one's past, and one's future — including rumination and worry (Raichle et al., 2001).

2. **Mind-wandering:** The DMN is active during spontaneous, undirected thought. While some mind-wandering is creative and beneficial, DMN hyperactivity is associated with rumination and depression (Hamilton et al., 2015).

3. **Rumination and the DMN:** Depressed individuals show increased DMN connectivity and difficulty deactivating it during tasks, which may explain the persistent, self-focused negative thinking characteristic of depression.

4. **Mindfulness and the DMN:** Meditation practice is associated with reduced DMN activity and increased connectivity between the DMN and executive control regions — suggesting that mindfulness training helps regulate the network (Brewer et al., 2011).

5. **Directed thought as intervention:** Structured reflection — prompts that direct thought toward specific, constructive questions — may help shift activity from default-mode rumination to executive-mode processing.
