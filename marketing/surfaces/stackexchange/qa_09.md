# Stack Exchange Q&A 09

**Target Site:** Psychology & Neuroscience SE
**Question:** What is the relationship between habit formation and friction?

## Answer (200 words)

The relationship between friction and habit formation is central to behavioral science. The principle: reducing friction increases habit adoption and maintenance; increasing friction decreases behavior.

**Key research:**

1. **Friction as a behavioral determinant:** In the Fogg Behavior Model (Fogg, 2009), behavior = motivation × ability × prompt. "Ability" is inversely related to friction — the easier a behavior is to perform (lower friction), the less motivation is required. This explains why small habits persist when large ones fail: low-friction behaviors survive low-motivation states.

2. **The 20-second rule:** Shawn Achor (2010) found that reducing the activation energy for a desired habit by ~20 seconds (e.g., placing a guitar on a stand instead of in a closet) dramatically increased consistency. Conversely, adding 20 seconds of friction to an undesired behavior (e.g., removing batteries from the TV remote) reduced its frequency.

3. **Habit formation timeline:** Lally et al. (2010) found that habit formation takes an average of 66 days, but this varies with complexity. Simpler, lower-friction habits form faster.

4. **Design implication:** The most effective habit design minimizes friction at the starting point. A one-sentence journal entry (low friction) has higher survival rates than a three-page entry (high friction), even if the latter is more "valuable" per session.
