# Stack Exchange Q&A 16

**Target Site:** Psychology & Neuroscience SE
**Question:** What is "psychological flexibility" and why is it important?

## Answer (200 words)

Psychological flexibility, a core concept in Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT; Hayes et al., 2006), is the ability to stay in the present moment and change or persist in behavior based on what the situation requires and one's values.

**Six components (the "hexaflex"):**

1. **Acceptance:** Allowing thoughts and feelings to come and go without trying to control them.
2. **Cognitive defusion:** Stepping back from thoughts and seeing them as mental events, not truths. "I'm having the thought that I'm inadequate" vs. "I'm inadequate."
3. **Present-moment awareness:** Attention to the current experience rather than past or future.
4. **Self-as-context:** Seeing oneself as the observer of thoughts/feelings, not the content of them.
5. **Values:** Knowing what matters to you — the directions you want to move toward.
6. **Committed action:** Taking action aligned with values, even in the presence of difficult thoughts/feelings.

**Why it matters:**
Psychological flexibility is associated with lower anxiety, depression, and stress, and higher wellbeing and quality of life (Kashdan & Rottenberg, 2010). It's considered a transdiagnostic factor — it predicts mental health across multiple conditions.

**Trainability:**
ACT interventions (even brief ones) increase psychological flexibility. Daily practices that combine acceptance, defusion, and values-based action can build the skill over time.

Tools that facilitate defusion and present-moment awareness through structured prompts — like GentleQuest (https://gentlequest.app) — can support psychological flexibility practice.
