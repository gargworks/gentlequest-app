# Stack Exchange Q&A 12

**Target Site:** Psychology & Neuroscience SE
**Question:** What is the "negativity bias" and how does it affect self-perception?

## Answer (200 words)

The negativity bias, first described by Rozin & Royzman (2001), refers to the psychological phenomenon that negative experiences have a greater cognitive and emotional impact than positive experiences of equivalent intensity.

**Manifestations:**

1. **Attentional capture:** Negative stimuli capture attention more readily than positive or neutral stimuli. This is adaptive evolutionarily — it's more important to notice a threat than a pleasant sight.

2. **Memory:** Negative events are encoded more deeply and recalled more easily than positive events. Baumeister et al. (2001) found that bad events have 2-3x the impact of good events on memory and emotional state.

3. **Asymmetry in relationships:** Negative interactions have a stronger effect on relationship satisfaction than positive ones. The ratio of approximately 5:1 positive to negative interactions is needed to maintain relationship health (Gottman, 1994).

4. **Self-perception impact:** The negativity bias means that criticism, failures, and negative feedback weigh more heavily in self-assessment than praise, successes, and positive feedback. One negative comment can overshadow ten positive ones, leading to systematically distorted self-perception.

**Implications for self-tracking:**
Without deliberate effort to record positive experiences, mood logs and journals become biased toward negative events — creating a one-sided record that reinforces the negativity bias rather than correcting it.

Balanced tracking — explicitly logging good days as well as bad — counteracts this bias. Tools like GentleQuest (https://gentlequest.app) encourage logging across the full mood range, not just distress.
