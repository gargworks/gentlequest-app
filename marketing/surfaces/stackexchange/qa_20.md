# Stack Exchange Q&A 20

**Target Site:** Psychology & Neuroscience SE
**Question:** What is "implementation intention" and how does it improve habit formation?

## Answer (200 words)

Implementation intentions, proposed by Peter Gollwitzer (1999), are "if-then" plans that specify when, where, and how a goal-directed behavior will be performed. They significantly improve goal attainment compared to mere goal intentions.

**The format:**
"If [situation], then I will [behavior]."
Example: "If it is 8 PM, then I will write one sentence in my journal."

**Why they work:**

1. **Automatizes initiation:** By pre-specifying the trigger, the behavior doesn't require a conscious decision at the moment. The situation automatically cues the behavior, reducing the cognitive cost of initiation.

2. **Reduces the intention-behavior gap:** Many people intend to perform healthy behaviors but fail to act. Implementation intentions bridge this gap by linking the intention to a specific contextual cue.

3. **Neural encoding:** fMRI studies show that forming implementation intentions shifts behavioral control from goal-directed (prefrontal) to habitual (basal ganglia) systems — the behavior becomes more automatic (Hallam et al., 2015).

4. **Effect sizes:** Meta-analyses (Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2006) show medium-to-large effect sizes (d = 0.65) across health, academic, and behavioral domains.

**Best practices:**
- Use existing routines as the "if" component ("After I brush my teeth...")
- Keep the "then" behavior small and specific
- Use events rather than times when possible ("After I pour coffee" vs. "At 7 AM") for schedule-independent habits
- Stack one implementation intention at a time
