## Body (600 words)

Sometimes the problem isn't too much emotion. It's none. The flat, grey, "nothing matters" state where everything feels muffled — like you're watching your life through glass. Emotional numbness is common, rarely talked about, and deeply frustrating when you're trying to maintain a reflection practice.

"How am I feeling?" returns nothing. "What did I notice?" returns nothing. The prompts that usually work feel like asking a dead phone for its battery percentage. There's no signal to report.

Here's the reframe: on numb days, the goal is not to generate feeling. The goal is to maintain the practice until the signal returns. And to do that, you need different prompts — prompts that don't require emotional access, only sensory or factual access.

**Prompt 1: "What did you eat today?"**
Not how you felt about it. Just what you ate. Breakfast, lunch, dinner, snacks. This prompt works because it's purely factual. You don't need to feel anything to answer it. But the act of answering keeps the journaling habit alive, and the food log often reveals patterns — skipped meals correlate with numbness, heavy meals correlate with afternoon dips — that become useful later.

**Prompt 2: "What was the temperature like?"**
Weather outside, temperature inside. Hot, cold, comfortable, drafty. This is a sensory prompt that requires zero emotional processing. It grounds you in the physical world without demanding that you feel anything about it. On numb days, sensory grounding is more accessible than emotional reflection.

**Prompt 3: "Who did you interact with today?"**
Not how the interactions felt. Just who they were. A barista, a coworker, a family member, a stranger on the train. This creates a social log — a record of human contact that doesn't require you to evaluate its quality. Over time, you may notice that numb days follow isolated days, or that certain people correlate with the return of feeling.

**Prompt 4: "What did you do with your hands today?"**
Typed, cooked, cleaned, held a cup, petted a dog, folded laundry. This is a body-awareness prompt that bypasses emotion entirely. It records physical engagement with the world — proof that you existed in a material way, even if the emotional dimension was offline.

**Prompt 5: "Rate your energy from 1 to 5."**
Not mood — energy. Energy is more accessible than mood during numb periods because it's a body sensation, not an emotional one. Even when you feel nothing emotionally, you can usually tell if your body is tired or alert. The energy rating maintains the data stream even when the mood stream goes quiet.

These prompts share a design principle: they're concrete, sensory, and low-affect. They don't ask "how do you feel?" because that question is broken on numb days. They ask "what happened?" and "what did you notice?" — questions that a depleted, numb mind can answer on autopilot.

The practice matters more than the content. A numb-day entry that says "ate toast, 62 degrees, talked to one person, typed a lot, energy 2/5" is a complete entry. It maintains the habit. It keeps the data stream unbroken. And when the signal returns — when you wake up one day and feelings are back — the practice is still running, ready to catch them.

Numbness is not a failure. It's a state. Like all states, it passes. The job of your reflection practice is not to fix the state but to survive it. These prompts are the survival kit.

Don't wait for feeling to return before you journal. Journal through the flatness. The entries will be short, factual, and unremarkable. That's fine. The archive doesn't need to be interesting. It needs to be continuous. And continuity is built one boring, numb-day entry at a time.

## This Week's Experiment
On any day you feel emotionally flat or numb, use the five sensory prompts instead of the usual reflection questions. One-word answers are fine. The goal is continuity, not depth.
