## Body (600 words)

The dominant digital wellness advice is nuclear. Delete social media. Get a dumbphone. Move to the woods. Go grayscale. Cancel every subscription.

This works for about a weekend. Then you reinstall everything, feel guilty about it, and conclude that digital wellness isn't for you.

The problem isn't that the nuclear approach is wrong — it's that it's unsustainable for most people. Most of us need our phones for work, communication, navigation, banking, and yes, some entertainment. The question isn't "how do I eliminate digital life?" It's "how do I layer small constraints that reduce the harm without requiring a lifestyle overhaul?"

Think of it as tiers. You pick the tier that matches your current capacity. No guilt about the tiers you don't reach.

**Tier 1: Five-Minute Setup (Do This Today)**
- Turn off all non-human notifications. App updates, promotional pushes, social media alerts — off. Keep calls, texts, and calendar.
- Set your phone to Do Not Disturb from 10 PM to 7 AM (adjust to your schedule).
- Move your three most distracting apps off the home screen — into a folder on the second page.
That's it. Five minutes. You've reduced daily notification interruptions by 80% without deleting anything.

**Tier 2: One Afternoon (Do This This Week)**
- Set app limits on your two biggest time-sink apps. Not zero — one hour each. You still get the app. You just get a boundary.
- Configure grayscale as a toggle (accessibility shortcut), not a permanent setting. Turn it on when you're doomscrolling; turn it off when you need color.
- Create a "first page" of your phone with only tools: camera, maps, calendar, notes, your journaling app. Everything else lives on page two or three.
- Set a recurring reminder at 9 PM: "Phone down soon." Not a command — a nudge.

**Tier 3: Weekend Project (Do This This Month)**
- Audit every app on your phone. Delete the ones you haven't opened in 30 days. Not the ones you "might need someday" — the ones you haven't actually used.
- Set up a charging station outside your bedroom. Phone charges in the kitchen, not on the nightstand. Buy a cheap alarm clock if you use your phone as an alarm.
- Configure a "focus mode" for work hours that hides all social apps automatically.
- Move your journaling and mood tracking to a single-purpose app. Not a notes app where everything lives — a dedicated space for reflection that you only open for that purpose.

The principle behind all three tiers: constraint, not elimination. You're not trying to remove digital life. You're trying to add friction to the parts that harm and reduce friction to the parts that help. Social media gets harder to reach. Your journaling app gets easier to reach. Same phone, different architecture.

The tiers are cumulative but not mandatory. If Tier 1 is all you ever do, that's fine. Tier 1 alone — turning off non-human notifications — has a measurable impact on daily stress and attention fragmentation. You don't need to reach Tier 3 to benefit. You need to pick the tier you'll actually maintain and do it.

The guilt trap: feeling bad that you haven't done "enough" digital wellness. There is no "enough." There's only your current capacity and the constraints that match it. A Tier 1 setup you maintain for a year beats a Tier 3 setup you abandon in a week. Consistency over intensity. We've been here before.

This week, do Tier 1. Five minutes. Turn off the notifications, set Do Not Disturb, move three apps. Then notice — over the next few days — how many fewer times your phone interrupts you. That difference is the beginning of digital wellness. Not a detox. Not a retreat. Just five minutes of configuration that changes the default.
