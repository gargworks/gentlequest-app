## Body (600 words)

Last week, you logged one number a day. Maybe you noticed something already: the number moves. A 7 on Tuesday. A 3 on Thursday. A 6 on Saturday. The temptation is to read each number as a judgment. A 7 means you're doing well. A 3 means you're failing.

You're not. A 3 is not a verdict. It's a weather report.

Think about how you treat actual weather. When it rains, you don't think the sky is broken. You don't feel personally responsible for the rain. You grab an umbrella and go about your day. You know the rain will pass. You know it's not permanent. You don't argue with it.

Your mood works the same way. It's a condition, not a conclusion. A 3 means there's a storm in your internal atmosphere right now. It doesn't mean your atmosphere is fundamentally broken. It doesn't mean you're a 3 person. It means today, in this moment, the weather is rough.

The problem is that we treat mood like a character trait instead of a condition. "I'm anxious" becomes "I'm an anxious person." "I'm sad today" becomes "I'm sad." The grammar collapses the temporary into the permanent. And once a mood feels permanent, it's hard to move through it.

The shift is linguistic but powerful. Try this: instead of "I'm anxious," say "I'm experiencing anxiety right now." Instead of "I'm in a bad mood," say "my mood is low at the moment." The first phrasing makes the mood an identity. The second makes it weather. Weather passes. Identities feel stuck.

This isn't denial. You're not pretending the bad mood doesn't exist. You're acknowledging it while refusing to make it a definition. "It's raining" and "it's a rainy planet" are very different statements, even though they describe the same moment.

When you log your mood in an app or a journal, you're creating a weather log. Over time, you'll see that 3s pass. That 7s come back. That no mood, good or bad, lasts forever. The archive becomes evidence that your internal climate is variable, not fixed. And that evidence is comforting in a way that reassurance alone can't be — because it's your data, from your life, proving the point.

There's a second benefit to the weather framing. It removes the urgency to fix every low mood. When a 3 shows up, you don't have to scramble to turn it into a 7. You can note it, let it be, and trust that the forecast will shift. Some moods need to be felt, not fixed. The weather report gives you permission to do exactly that.

So this week, keep logging your number. But change how you read it. When you see a low number, say to yourself: "That's weather. Not a verdict." When you see a high number, enjoy it without clinging to it. "That's weather too." The goal isn't to have all 8s and 9s. The goal is to observe the full range without panicking about any single data point.

Your mood is a sky. Sometimes clear, sometimes cloudy, sometimes stormy. The sky is always fine, even when the weather isn't. You are the sky. The mood is just what's happening in it right now.

## This Week's Experiment
Keep logging your daily mood number. Each time you log, say or think: "This is weather, not a verdict." Notice if the framing changes how you feel about low numbers.
