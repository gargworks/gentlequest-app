## Body (600 words)

It's 6 PM on Sunday. You feel it — the tightness in your chest, the mental rehearsal of everything that could go wrong, the vague dread that the weekend is ending and Monday is coming. The Sunday scaries.

They're not a character flaw. They're not weakness. They're anticipatory anxiety — your brain running threat simulations about the upcoming week. And without a closing ritual, those simulations loop. The dread doesn't resolve; it just spins.

The fix isn't to suppress the anxiety. We've already covered why suppression backfires. The fix is to give the anxiety structure — a container that channels the looping energy into something useful. A Sunday reset ritual.

It takes twelve minutes. Three steps. Same time every Sunday.

**Step 1: The Week Ahead Preview (5 minutes)**
Open your calendar. Look at the upcoming week. Don't plan — just look. Identify one anchor per day: the one event or task that defines that day. Monday's anchor might be a team meeting. Wednesday's might be a deadline. Friday's might be dinner with a friend. You're not creating a schedule. You're reducing uncertainty. The brain's main fuel for anxiety is the unknown. By previewing the week, you replace "what's going to happen?" with "here's what's going to happen." The simulations slow down because the variables are now visible.

**Step 2: The Weekend Closing Entry (5 minutes)**
Write one sentence that closes the weekend. Not a summary — a closing. "This weekend I rested." "This weekend I saw friends." "This weekend I did nothing and it was enough." The sentence creates a psychological boundary. Without it, the weekend bleeds into the week with no clear ending, and your brain doesn't register that rest happened. The closing entry is the period at the end of the sentence. It tells your mind: that chapter is done.

**Step 3: The Monday Morning Choice (2 minutes)**
Pick one thing about tomorrow that you're choosing — not dreading. It doesn't have to be exciting. "I'm choosing to have a good coffee before the meeting." "I'm choosing to walk the long way to the office." "I'm choosing to start with the easy task." This step restores agency. Anticipatory anxiety thrives on the feeling that things are happening to you. The Monday choice reminds you that you're still making decisions, even within a structured week.

Twelve minutes. Preview, close, choose. The anxiety doesn't disappear — it's not supposed to. But it gets a container. Instead of a free-floating dread that lasts from 6 PM to midnight, it becomes a structured twelve-minute process that ends with a clear next step.

The ritual works because it addresses the three drivers of Sunday anxiety: uncertainty (fixed by the preview), lack of closure (fixed by the closing entry), and loss of agency (fixed by the Monday choice). You're not fighting the anxiety. You're giving it somewhere to go.

Do it at the same time every Sunday. Anchor it to something — after dinner, before your shower, when you set your alarm. The consistency matters. After three or four weeks, your brain will start to recognize the ritual as the signal that the weekend is ending and the week is beginning. The transition becomes intentional instead of accidental.

This week, try the twelve-minute Sunday reset. Preview the week. Close the weekend. Choose one thing about Monday. That's it. Not a life overhaul. Just a boundary, drawn once a week, that gives your mind permission to transition.
