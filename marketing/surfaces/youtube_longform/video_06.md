# Video 06 — YouTube Long-Form Brief

**Title:** The Evening Reflection Routine That Doesn't Require a Journal
**Target Audience:** People who want to reflect on their day but resist traditional journaling. Evening people, night owls, and anyone whose brain is too tired for long-form writing at night.

## Script Outline (6 minutes)

### Hook (0:00–0:40)
"It's 10 PM. Your brain is fried. Someone tells you to 'journal about your day' and you want to throw the notebook across the room. Here's the evening reflection routine for people who are too tired to reflect."

### Act 1: Why Evening Reflection Fails (0:40–2:00)
- Decision fatigue: by evening, you've made 35,000 choices. "What did I learn today?" is one too many.
- The blank page problem: open-ended questions require creative energy you don't have
- The performance trap: reflection becomes a productivity review, not a wind-down
- The timing error: people try to reflect right before sleep, when the brain is shutting down

### Act 2: The Five-Part Check-In (2:00–4:15)
- Prompt 1: "What was one small thing that went slightly better than expected?" (Low bar, specific)
- Prompt 2: "Rate your energy from 1–5." (No judgment, just a number)
- Prompt 3: "Name one emotion you felt today." (One word, not a paragraph)
- Prompt 4: "Is there anything on your mind right now?" (Brain dump, one sentence max)
- Prompt 5: "What's one thing you're letting go of for tonight?" (Release, not resolution)
- Total time: under 3 minutes
- Show app: screen recording of the five-prompt sequence in GentleQuest

### Act 3: How to Make It Automatic (4:15–5:30)
- Set a recurring reminder for 30 minutes before your usual bedtime
- Keep your phone within arm's reach — don't require getting up
- Use the same five prompts every night — predictability reduces friction
- The "one word counts" rule: if all you can manage is "tired," that's a complete entry

### Outro / CTA (5:30–6:00)
- "GentleQuest has this exact five-prompt sequence built in. Links below."
- Platform links
- "This is not a diagnosis. Please see a qualified professional for clinical concerns."
