### Hook (0:00–0:50)

### Act 1: The Ironic Process Theory (0:50–3:00)
- Daniel Wegner's white bear experiment: try not to think about a white bear. You think about it more.
- The brain has two systems: the monitoring system (checking for the unwanted thought) and the operating system (trying to think of something else)
- The monitoring system runs automatically and faster than the operating system
- Result: suppressing a thought makes it more accessible
- Applied to anxiety: "don't think negative thoughts" → you think more negative thoughts
- Add shame: now you're anxious AND failing at positivity

### Act 2: What Works Instead (3:00–5:30)
- Step 1: Name the thought — "I'm having the thought that everything will go wrong"
- Step 2: Allow it — don't fight, don't fix, just let it sit
- Step 3: Gently redirect — not to "positive thinking" but to present-moment attention
- The difference between suppression and redirection: suppression fights, redirection flows
- Show app: screen recording of a mood entry where someone logs "anxious" without judgment

### Act 3: Building a Daily Practice (5:30–7:00)
- The naming habit: once a day, name one difficult emotion without trying to change it
- The allowing habit: sit with the named emotion for 60 seconds
- The redirecting habit: after 60 seconds, shift to one concrete action in the present
- Why this compounds: over weeks, the brain learns that emotions are temporary and survivable
- The goal is not happiness. The goal is honesty — which is more sustainable than forced optimism.
