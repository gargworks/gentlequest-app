# Video 03 — YouTube Long-Form Brief

**Title:** Why Your Anxiety Spikes on Sunday Evening (and How to Fix It)
**Target Audience:** Working adults aged 25–45 who experience Sunday evening anxiety. People who want practical, non-clinical strategies for managing anticipatory stress.

## Script Outline (8 minutes)

### Hook (0:00–0:50)
"It's 6 PM on Sunday. You feel it — the tightness, the dread, the mental rehearsal of everything that could go wrong this week. It has a name. It's predictable. And there's a simple ritual that interrupts it."

### Act 1: What Sunday Scaries Actually Are (0:50–3:00)
- It's not a character flaw — it's anticipatory anxiety
- Your brain runs threat simulations about the upcoming week
- Without a closing ritual for the weekend, the simulations loop
- The role of uncertainty: the brain prefers known threats to unknown ones
- Why it peaks Sunday evening: the transition from autonomy to obligation
- Brief note: this is common and not a diagnosis. See a professional if it's severe.

### Act 2: The Sunday Reset Ritual (3:00–5:30)
- Step 1: The Week Ahead Preview (5 min) — look at calendar, identify one anchor per day
- Step 2: The Weekend Closing Entry (5 min) — one sentence: "This weekend I..."
- Step 3: The Monday Morning Choice (2 min) — pick one thing about tomorrow that you're choosing, not dreading
- Total: 12 minutes. Do it at the same time every Sunday.
- Show app: screen recording of the evening reflection prompt sequence

### Act 3: Why This Works (5:30–7:00)
- The preview reduces uncertainty (the brain's main fuel for anxiety)
- The closing entry creates a psychological boundary between weekend and week
- The Monday choice restores a sense of agency
- It's not about eliminating anxiety — it's about giving it structure

### Outro / CTA (7:00–8:00)
- "I use GentleQuest for the closing entry — it has a built-in evening reflection sequence. Links below."
- Platform links on screen
- "This is not a diagnosis. If your Sunday anxiety is severe or affecting sleep, please see a qualified professional."
- "Subscribe for more quiet experiments."
