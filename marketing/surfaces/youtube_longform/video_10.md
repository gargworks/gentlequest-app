# Video 10 — YouTube Long-Form Brief

**Title:** The Weekly Review for Mental Health (Not Productivity)
**Target Audience:** People who do weekly reviews for work but never for their own wellbeing. Productivity enthusiasts looking to apply review frameworks to mental health.

## Script Outline (6 minutes)

### Hook (0:00–0:40)
"You review your work every week. Your tasks, your projects, your goals. But when was the last time you reviewed your own mental state? Here's a fifteen-minute weekly review designed for wellbeing, not output."

### Act 1: The Productivity Review Problem (0:40–2:00)
- Standard weekly reviews ask: what did I accomplish? What's next? What's blocked?
- The implicit message: your week is measured by output
- What's missing: how did you feel? What drained you? What gave you energy?
- The result: you optimize for productivity and wonder why you're burned out
- The fix: a parallel review that measures wellbeing, not output

### Act 2: The Five-Section Wellbeing Review (2:00–4:30)
- Section 1: Mood Summary — look at the week's mood entries, identify the high and low
- Section 2: Energy Audit — what drained you? What fueled you? (one of each, minimum)
- Section 3: Pattern Noticed — any recurring theme across the week?
- Section 4: One Intention — not a goal, a quality: "more quiet," "fewer meetings," "earlier walks"
- Section 5: One Thing to Let Go Of — something from this week you're releasing
- Total time: 15 minutes. Once a week. Same time, same place.
- Show app: screen recording of the weekly review view in GentleQuest

### Act 3: Why This Compounds (4:30–5:45)
- Week 1: you learn nothing new. Week 4: you see your first pattern. Week 12: you know yourself.
- The archive effect: after three months, you can compare weeks and see what actually changed
- The release ritual: "letting go of" one thing per week prevents emotional accumulation
- This is not therapy. It's a weather report. But weather reports, taken weekly, become climate data.

### Outro / CTA (5:45–6:00)
- "GentleQuest has a built-in weekly review with these five sections. Links below."
- Platform links
- "This is not a diagnosis. Please see a qualified professional for clinical concerns."
