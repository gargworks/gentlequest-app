# Video 05 — YouTube Long-Form Brief

**Title:** How to Track Your Mood Without Making Yourself Miserable
**Target Audience:** People interested in mood tracking who have tried it and quit due to guilt, anxiety about the data, or the "tracking makes me feel worse" problem.

## Script Outline (6 minutes)

### Hook (0:00–0:40)
"You log a bad mood. Then you feel bad about having a bad mood. Then you feel worse for not logging yesterday. This is the guilt spiral that kills mood tracking. Here's how to break it."

### Act 1: The Guilt Spiral (0:40–2:30)
- Step 1: You log a 3/10. The number feels like a verdict.
- Step 2: You judge yourself for the number.
- Step 3: You skip a day because logging feels like admitting things are bad.
- Step 4: You feel guilty for skipping.
- Step 5: You abandon tracking entirely.
- The core error: treating a mood entry as an evaluation instead of an observation

### Act 2: Three Reframing Techniques (2:30–4:45)
- Technique 1: Naming — "I'm logging a 3, not failing at life"
- Technique 2: Neutralizing — treat it like a weather report, not a report card
- Technique 3: Normalizing — "a 3 is data, not a diagnosis. Everyone has 3s."
- Show app: screen recording of mood entry with neutral language, no judgment prompts
- The "skip without shame" rule: missing a day doesn't reset anything meaningful

### Act 3: Sustainable Tracking (4:45–5:45)
- Track for patterns, not for judgment
- Review weekly, not daily — daily review breeds rumination
- The "good day minimum": log the 8s and 9s too
- Your data is for you, not for a doctor or a leaderboard

### Outro / CTA (5:45–6:00)
- "GentleQuest's mood tracker is designed to be judgment-free. No streaks to break, no scores to beat. Links below."
- Platform links
- "This is not a diagnosis. Please see a qualified professional for clinical concerns."
