# Video 09 — YouTube Long-Form Brief

**Title:** How to Build a Habit That Survives Your Worst Week
**Target Audience:** People who start habits during good weeks and abandon them during bad weeks. Anyone interested in resilient habit design.

## Script Outline (7 minutes)

### Hook (0:00–0:45)
"Every habit guide assumes you're having a good week. But the real test of a habit isn't whether you do it on a good day. It's whether it survives the week everything falls apart. Here's how to design for your worst day, not your best."

### Act 1: The Good Week Fallacy (0:45–2:30)
- We design habits when we feel motivated (Monday, New Year, after a breakup)
- Motivated-us creates habits that motivated-us can do — but unmotivated-us has to live with them
- The "ceiling design" problem: you build for your best day, then fail on your average day
- The dropout data: most habits die in the first bad week, not the first good one
- The insight: if your habit survives your worst week, it survives forever

### Act 2: Designing for the Floor (2:30–5:00)
- Principle 1: Shrink before stress hits — don't wait for a bad week to reduce the habit
- Principle 2: Remove all optional steps — a habit with optional parts will shed them under stress, then collapse
- Principle 3: Pre-write the "bad day version" — the minimum viable entry you can do at midnight after a terrible day
- Example: Full habit = 5-prompt reflection. Bad day version = one mood rating. Both count.
- Show app: screen recording of the "bad day" one-tap mood entry

### Act 3: The Survival Protocol (5:00–6:15)
- Week 1: Do the full version every day
- Week 2: Deliberately do the minimum version on two days (practice the floor)
- Week 3: Notice which version you actually gravitate toward — that's your real habit
- Week 4: Set the floor as your default, the full version as a bonus
- The mindset shift: "I did the minimum" = success, not failure

- Platform links
