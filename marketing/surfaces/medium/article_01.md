## Teaser (100 words)
Most journaling advice tells you to fill a page. That advice fails by day four. The two-minute journal flips the rule: write one sentence about your morning, one about your mood, and one thing you noticed. That's it. The barrier drops so low that skipping feels harder than doing it. After thirty days, you have a quiet record of your inner weather — not a literary masterpiece, but something more useful: proof that you showed up. This piece breaks down why micro-entries outperform long-form journaling for consistency, and how to set up a friction-free prompt cycle.
