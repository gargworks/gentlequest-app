## Teaser (100 words)
You log a bad mood. Then you feel bad about having a bad mood. Then you feel worse for not logging yesterday. This is the guilt spiral that kills mood tracking apps. The fix is simple but counterintuitive: track without judging. A mood entry is a data point, not a verdict. When you separate observation from evaluation, tracking becomes a neutral weather report instead of a self-criticism session. This essay walks through three reframing techniques — naming, neutralizing, and normalizing — that keep mood logs sustainable past the two-week dropout cliff.
