# Quora Answer 13

**Question:** How do I stop being so hard on myself?
**Target Quora Topic:** Mental Health, Self Improvement
**Link Placement:** Bottom of answer, natural context

## Answer (300 words)

Being hard on yourself isn't a personality trait — it's a learned pattern, and like any pattern, it can be interrupted. Here's what actually works:

**1. Self-compassion is a behavior, not a feeling.**
Most people wait to feel self-compassion before practicing it. That's backwards. The feeling follows the behavior, not the other way around. The protocol: when you notice self-criticism, do three things — acknowledge the difficulty ("this is hard"), remind yourself struggle is universal ("other people feel this way too"), and offer yourself the words you'd give a friend. You don't have to believe the words. The repetition is what rewires the pattern.

**2. Catch the critic in the act.**
Self-criticism runs on autopilot — you don't choose it, it just fires. The first step is noticing it. "I'm having the thought that I'm not good enough." Not "I'm not good enough" — but "I'm having the thought that..." This linguistic shift creates distance between you and the critic. You're not the thought; you're the one observing it.

**3. Lower the bar.**
A lot of self-criticism comes from a gap between your standards and your current capacity. If your standard is perfection and your capacity is 60%, you'll always feel like you're failing. Right-size your expectations to match your actual capacity on any given day. A 60% effort you actually make beats a 100% standard you never meet.

**4. Track the pattern.**
When does the self-criticism peak? After work? On Sundays? When you're tired? Tracking your mood alongside self-critical episodes reveals triggers, and triggers give you leverage. You can't fix what you can't see.

The goal isn't to never criticize yourself. Some self-criticism is useful. The goal is to turn the volume down from a constant roar to an occasional, quiet signal you can choose whether to listen to.

*This is not a diagnosis. If self-criticism is severe, persistent, or affecting your mental health, please see a qualified professional.*
