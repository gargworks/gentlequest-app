# Quora Answer 20

**Question:** How do I practice mindfulness when my mind won't stop?
**Target Quora Topic:** Mindfulness, Meditation
**Link Placement:** Bottom of answer, natural context

## Answer (300 words)

The biggest misconception about mindfulness is that it requires a quiet mind. It doesn't. A busy mind doing mindfulness is still doing mindfulness. Here's how to practice when your thoughts won't stop:

**1. Stop trying to stop the thoughts.**
The instruction "clear your mind" is counterproductive. Trying not to think triggers the ironic process — your brain's monitoring system checks for the unwanted thought, which activates it. Instead, let the thoughts be there. Your job isn't to empty the mind; it's to change your relationship to what's in it.

**2. Notice, name, return.**
This is the core mindfulness loop:
- Notice: "I'm thinking about work."
- Name: "That's a thought about work." (Not "I'm anxious about work" — just "that's a thought.")
- Return: Bring attention back to your breath, your body, or the present moment.
The thoughts will come back. That's fine. Notice, name, return. Again and again. The returning IS the practice. Each return is a rep. You're building the muscle of attention redirection, not the muscle of thought elimination.

**3. Start with 60 seconds, not 20 minutes.**
A 60-second mindfulness practice you actually do beats a 20-minute session you skip. Set a timer for one minute. Notice, name, return for 60 seconds. That's a complete practice. Expand later if you want, but only after 60 seconds is automatic.

**4. Use sensory anchors.**
When thoughts are loud, sensory attention is more accessible than breath attention. Feel your feet on the floor. Notice the temperature of the air. Listen to the sounds in the room. Sensory input is in the present moment; thoughts are in the past or future. Sensory anchoring pulls you to now.

**5. Track the pattern.**
When is your mind busiest? Morning? Evening? After screens? Knowing your pattern helps you choose the right time for practice.
