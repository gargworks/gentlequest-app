# Quora Answer 19

**Question:** How do I deal with imposter syndrome?
**Target Quora Topic:** Self Improvement, Career
**Link Placement:** Bottom of answer, natural context

## Answer (300 words)

Imposter syndrome — the persistent feeling that you're not as competent as others perceive you to be — is extremely common, especially among high achievers. Here's a practical approach:

**1. Understand what it actually is.**
Imposter syndrome isn't a sign that you're inadequate. It's a sign that your internal self-assessment is miscalibrated. You compare your internal experience (which includes all your doubts, mistakes, and uncertainty) to other people's external presentation (which shows only confidence and competence). The comparison is structurally unfair. Everyone feels uncertain internally. You just can't see theirs.

**2. Collect evidence — don't argue with feelings.**
You can't reason your way out of imposter syndrome by telling yourself "I'm actually good." The feeling doesn't respond to logic. What it does respond to is evidence. Keep a record of concrete accomplishments, positive feedback, and moments of competence. Not a mental list — a written one. When the feeling spikes, review the evidence. Not to argue with the feeling, but to contextualize it.

**3. Normalize the feeling.**
Imposter syndrome thrives in isolation — the belief that "everyone else has it figured out and I'm the only fraud." Naming it publicly (even to one trusted person) breaks the isolation. "I'm having imposter syndrome thoughts right now." The naming creates distance. You're not the thought; you're the one observing it.

**4. Track the pattern.**
When does imposter syndrome peak? After promotions? Before presentations? On certain days? Tracking reveals triggers, and triggers give you leverage. If it always spikes before presentations, you can prepare a specific pre-presentation protocol.

**5. Act despite the feeling.**
The goal isn't to eliminate imposter syndrome before acting. It's to act while feeling it. "I feel like a fraud and I'm going to give this presentation anyway." The action builds the evidence. The evidence, over time, recalibrates the self-assessment.

*This is not a diagnosis. If imposter syndrome is significantly affecting your wellbeing or career, please see a qualified professional.*
