# Quora Answer 37

**Question:** How do I deal with perfectionism?
**Target Quora Topic:** Psychology, Self Improvement
**Link Placement:** Bottom of answer, natural context

## Answer (300 words)

Perfectionism isn't a virtue — it's a defense mechanism. It's the belief that if you do everything perfectly, you can avoid criticism, failure, and the painful emotions that come with them. Here's how to dismantle it:

**1. Understand the cost.**
Perfectionism doesn't produce better work — it produces slower work, more procrastination, and more burnout. The "perfect" standard is never actually reached (because perfection is subjective and moving), so you either ship late, ship less, or don't ship at all. The cost is not just time — it's the projects you never started, the ideas you never shared, the life you never lived because it wasn't "ready."

**2. Separate the standard from the goal.**
The standard is your internal quality bar. The goal is getting it done. These are different things. Perfectionism conflates them: "If it's not perfect, it's not done." Decouple them: "The goal is done. The standard is good enough for this context." A report that's 85% perfect and submitted on time is more valuable than one that's 95% perfect and two weeks late.

**3. Practice shipping imperfect things.**
Start small. Send an email without re-reading it three times. Post something without editing it to death. Share an idea before it's fully formed. Each act of shipping imperfect work builds tolerance for imperfection. The world doesn't end. The tolerance compounds.

**4. Right-size the standard to the context.**
Not everything deserves the same quality bar. A casual email to a colleague doesn't need the same standard as a client presentation. A personal journal entry doesn't need the same standard as a published article. Perfectionism applies one standard to everything; healthy work applies the right standard to the right context.

**5. Track the perfectionism pattern.**
When does perfectionism peak? On certain types of tasks? When you're tired? When the stakes feel high? Knowing your pattern lets you catch it earlier and apply the right-size standard before you spiral.
