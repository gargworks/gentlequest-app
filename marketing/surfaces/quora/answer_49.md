# Quora Answer 49

**Question:** How do I stop negative self-talk?
**Target Quora Topic:** Mental Health, Self Improvement
**Link Placement:** Bottom of answer, natural context

## Answer (300 words)

Negative self-talk runs on autopilot — you don't choose it, it just fires. You can't delete it, but you can change your relationship to it. Here's how:

**1. Catch it in the act.**
The first step is noticing. Most negative self-talk is so habitual it's invisible — it feels like truth, not opinion. Start by simply catching it: "I just told myself I'm stupid." Not "I am stupid" — "I just told myself I am stupid." The linguistic shift creates distance. You're not the thought; you're the observer of the thought.

**2. Name the pattern, not the content.**
Instead of engaging with what the self-talk says ("Am I actually stupid?"), name the pattern: "That's my inner critic." Or "That's a self-attack thought." Naming the pattern as a pattern — rather than as truth — reduces its power. You're categorizing it as noise, not signal.

**3. Ask: would I say this to a friend?**
If a friend made the same mistake, would you say "You're so stupid, you always ruin everything"? No. You'd say "That didn't go well, but you'll figure it out." The gap between how you talk to yourself and how you talk to friends is the gap where self-compassion lives. Close it.

**4. Don't argue with the thought — replace the channel.**
Arguing with negative self-talk ("No, I'm not stupid!") gives it more attention and energy. Instead, treat it like a radio station you don't like — don't argue with the DJ, just change the channel. Redirect to a concrete action: a task, a conversation, a walk. The thought doesn't need to be resolved; it needs to be outcompeted by something else.

**5. Track when it peaks.**
Negative self-talk isn't constant — it has triggers. When tired? After social events? Before deadlines? Track your mood and note when the self-critic is loudest. Knowing your triggers lets you prepare targeted responses.

*This is not a diagnosis. If negative self-talk is severe, persistent, or affecting your mental health, please see a qualified professional.*
