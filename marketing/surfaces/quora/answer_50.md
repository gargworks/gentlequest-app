# Quora Answer 50

**Question:** How do I maintain my mental health during a major life transition?
**Target Quora Topic:** Mental Health, Life
**Link Placement:** Bottom of answer, natural context

## Answer (300 words)

Major life transitions — a new job, a move, a breakup, a loss, becoming a parent — are inherently destabilizing. Even positive transitions create stress. Here's how to maintain your mental health through them:

**1. Lower your expectations of yourself.**
During a transition, your cognitive bandwidth is consumed by the change itself. You won't have the same capacity for work, socializing, or self-improvement. This is temporary and normal. Lower the bar on everything non-essential. The minimum viable version of your routines is enough. A one-sentence journal entry. A five-minute walk. A single meal. The floor keeps the structure intact.

**2. Maintain the anchors.**
When everything is changing, identify 2–3 things that aren't. A morning coffee. A daily call with a friend. A bedtime routine. These anchors provide stability when the rest of life is in flux. They're not ambitious — they're reliable. Reliability is what your nervous system needs during uncertainty.

**3. Track your mood to distinguish transition stress from something bigger.**
Transitions cause mood dips — that's expected. But how do you know if what you're feeling is normal transition stress or something that needs professional attention? Track your mood daily. If the dip lasts more than 3–4 weeks after the transition settles, or if it deepens instead of stabilizing, that's worth paying attention to.

**4. Don't make additional big decisions.**
During a transition, your decision-making is compromised. Don't add more major changes on top. No career pivots, no relationship decisions, no big purchases. Let the current transition settle before initiating the next one.

**5. Talk to people who've been through it.**
Whatever transition you're experiencing, someone has been through it before. Find them. Not for advice — for normalization. "Yes, the first three months are disorienting. That's normal." Normalization reduces the anxiety that comes from thinking you're doing it wrong.

**6. Give it time.**
Most transitions take 3–6 months to feel settled. Not 3–6 weeks. If you're expecting to feel "normal" in week two, you'll think something's wrong. It's not wrong. It's just early.
