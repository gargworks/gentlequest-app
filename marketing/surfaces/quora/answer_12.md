# Quora Answer 12

**Question:** Why can't I sleep even when I'm tired?
**Target Quora Topic:** Sleep, Mental Health
**Link Placement:** Bottom of answer, natural context

## Answer (300 words)

Being physically tired and being able to sleep are two different systems. Your body can be exhausted while your brain is fully activated. Here's why this happens and what to do:

**1. The cortisol curve is off.**
If you've been under stress, your cortisol rhythm can shift — elevated in the evening when it should be dropping. This means your body is tired but your nervous system is in "alert" mode. Fix: morning sunlight exposure (10–15 minutes outside within an hour of waking) helps reset the cortisol curve. It's boring but it works.

**2. Revenge bedtime procrastination.**
If your daytime feels out of your control — packed schedule, demanding job, no personal time — your brain tries to reclaim autonomy at night by staying up. It's not that you can't sleep; it's that you're refusing to end the day because the day didn't include enough "you." Fix: build in 20 minutes of genuine personal time during the day so the evening doesn't carry that burden.

**3. The mental loop.**
You lie down and your brain starts processing the day — and planning tomorrow, and replaying that conversation, and worrying about next week. Fix: do a "brain dump" 30 minutes before bed. Write down everything on your mind. Get it out of your head and onto paper (or an app). Then do a short closing reflection — one sentence that puts a period on the day. "Today is done."

**4. Phone in bed.**
The blue light suppresses melatonin, but the bigger issue is cognitive activation — you're reading things that trigger emotions right before sleep. Fix: charge your phone outside the bedroom. Use a real alarm clock.

The key insight: sleep isn't just about tiredness. It's about whether your nervous system has received the signal that the day is over and the threat has passed. Give it that signal.

*This is not a diagnosis. If sleep problems persist for weeks, please see a qualified professional — chronic insomnia deserves proper medical evaluation.*
