# Quora Answer 17

**Question:** How do I deal with burnout?
**Target Quora Topic:** Burnout, Mental Health
**Link Placement:** Bottom of answer, natural context

## Answer (300 words)

Burnout isn't just being tired. It's a state of emotional, physical, and cognitive exhaustion caused by prolonged stress without adequate recovery. Here's what actually helps:

**1. Stop treating rest as a reward.**
The biggest burnout trap: "I'll rest when I finish this project / hit this deadline / get through this week." Rest becomes something you earn, which means it never happens because there's always a next deadline. Rest is not a reward for finishing — it's a requirement for functioning. Schedule it like you schedule meetings. Non-negotiable.

**2. Audit your energy drains.**
For one week, track what drains you and what fuels you. Not vaguely — specifically. "The 3-hour Wednesday meeting drained me." "The morning walk fueled me." You'll see patterns. Burnout is often caused by 2–3 specific recurring drains, not by "everything." If you can reduce or restructure even one of them, the load lightens.

**3. Lower your standards temporarily.**
Burnout is not the time to optimize. It's the time to survive. Cut non-essential commitments. Do the minimum viable version of everything. A 50% workout you do beats a 100% workout you skip. A one-sentence journal entry beats no entry. The goal is to reduce cognitive load, not to add new self-improvement projects.

**4. Rebuild the recovery buffer.**
Burnout means your recovery buffer is empty — you've been spending more energy than you're replenishing. Rebuilding takes time. It's not one weekend off; it's weeks of consistent, small recovery actions: sleep, sunlight, movement, genuine downtime (not phone scrolling).

**5. Track your energy, not just your mood.**
Mood and energy are different systems. You can have a decent mood with zero energy — that's a burnout signature. Track both daily. If energy stays low for weeks despite rest, that's important data.

Burnout recovery is not a hack. It's a process of systematically reducing load and increasing recovery until the buffer rebuilds. It takes longer than you want. But it works.

*This is not a diagnosis. Burnout can overlap with depression and anxiety. If symptoms persist or worsen, please see a qualified professional.*
