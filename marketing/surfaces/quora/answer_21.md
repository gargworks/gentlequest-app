# Quora Answer 21

**Question:** How do I help a friend who is struggling with anxiety?
**Target Quora Topic:** Anxiety, Mental Health
**Link Placement:** Bottom of answer, natural context

## Answer (300 words)

Supporting a friend with anxiety can feel helpless, but there are specific things that genuinely help:

**1. Don't try to fix it.**
The most common mistake is offering solutions: "Have you tried meditation?" "You should exercise more." "Just think positive." These are well-meant but counterproductive. Anxiety isn't a problem to be solved in conversation. It's a state to be supported through. Your job isn't to fix; it's to be present.

**2. Listen and reflect, don't advise.**
When they're anxious, let them talk. Reflect back what you hear: "That sounds really overwhelming." "It makes sense that you're worried about that." Validation — confirming that their experience is real and makes sense — reduces the shame that often accompanies anxiety. Shame says "I shouldn't feel this way." Validation says "it makes sense that you do."

**3. Ask what they need.**
"What would help right now — talking, distraction, or just sitting here?" This gives them agency, which anxiety strips away. Sometimes they don't know — that's fine. Offer options: "Want to go for a walk? Want me to just be here? Want to talk about something else?"

**4. Don't say "calm down."**
"Calm down" has never calmed anyone down. It communicates that their anxiety is a burden and they should suppress it. Instead: "I'm here. Take your time. There's no rush."

**5. Follow up, but don't monitor.**
A text the next day — "thinking of you, no need to reply" — is powerful. It shows they're in your mind without creating an obligation. Don't check in multiple times a day or ask "how's your anxiety?" That turns you into a monitor, which adds pressure.

**6. Encourage professional support — gently.**
If their anxiety is persistent or severe, suggest professional help. Not as a command — as an option. "Have you ever talked to someone about this? I've heard it can really help." You're planting a seed, not making a referral.

For your own wellbeing: supporting someone with anxiety can be draining. Track your own mood and energy so you know when you need to recharge. I use GentleQuest — a private mood tracker that helps me notice when I'm running low. iOS, Android, web: https://gentlequest.app

*This is not a diagnosis. If your friend's anxiety is severe, encourage them to see a qualified professional. If they're in crisis, contact emergency services.*
