# Quora Answer 23

**Question:** How do I stop procrastinating when I'm overwhelmed?
**Target Quora Topic:** Productivity, Psychology
**Link Placement:** Bottom of answer, natural context

## Answer (300 words)

Procrastination when overwhelmed isn't laziness — it's a stress response. When a task feels too big, ambiguous, or threatening (to your self-worth, your time, your energy), your brain's threat-detection system flags it and you avoid it. The fix is to reduce the threat level of the task:

**1. Shrink the task until it's non-threatening.**
"Write the report" is overwhelming. "Open the document and write the title" is not. The brain doesn't resist small, concrete actions — it resists big, ambiguous ones. Define the smallest possible first step. Not the whole task — the first 2 minutes of it. Once you start, momentum usually carries you further. But the starting step must be tiny enough that your brain doesn't flag it as a threat.

**2. Lower the quality bar.**
A lot of procrastination is perfectionism in disguise. You're not avoiding the task — you're avoiding doing it imperfectly. Give yourself permission to do a bad version. A terrible first draft. A messy outline. A ugly prototype. You can edit bad work. You can't edit no work. The first version is allowed to be garbage.

**3. Separate planning from doing.**
When you're overwhelmed, trying to plan and execute simultaneously creates cognitive overload. Do them separately. Planning: spend 5 minutes breaking the task into 3–5 micro-steps. Doing: pick one micro-step and do only that. Don't think about the other steps while doing the first one.

**4. Use a 5-minute timer.**
Set a timer for 5 minutes. Work on the shrunken task for 5 minutes. When the timer goes off, you can stop. No obligation to continue. Most of the time, you'll continue because the hardest part was starting. If you stop, that's fine — you did 5 minutes, which is more than zero.

**5. Track the pattern.**
When do you procrastinate most? Morning? After lunch? When tired? When a specific type of task? Tracking reveals your procrastination triggers.

*This is not a diagnosis. If procrastination is chronic and affecting your life significantly, please see a qualified professional — it can overlap with ADHD or anxiety.*
