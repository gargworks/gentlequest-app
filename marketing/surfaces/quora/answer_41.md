# Quora Answer 41

**Question:** How do I deal with grief?
**Target Quora Topic:** Grief, Mental Health
**Link Placement:** Bottom of answer, natural context

## Answer (300 words)

Grief is one of the hardest human experiences, and there's no shortcut through it. But there are ways to support the process:

**1. Grief doesn't follow stages.**
The "five stages of grief" (denial, anger, bargaining, depression, acceptance) are widely misunderstood. They were never meant to be linear. Grief comes in waves — you might feel acceptance one morning and anger that afternoon. That's not regression; that's how grief works. Don't try to schedule it or rush it.

**2. Let the waves come.**
Grief waves are unpredictable — a song, a smell, a date, a random thought can trigger one. When a wave comes, let it. Don't suppress it, don't distract from it, don't judge yourself for it. Cry, sit with it, feel it. Waves pass. Fighting them makes them last longer.

**3. Maintain the floor.**
Grief drains energy. Your basic routines — eating, sleeping, moving — may feel impossible. Lower the bar. The minimum viable version of every routine is enough. A five-minute walk. A single meal. One sentence in a journal. The goal during grief is not thriving; it's maintaining the floor so the structure holds.

**4. Talk about it — or don't.**
Some people process grief by talking. Others need quiet. Both are valid. Don't let anyone pressure you into "opening up" if that's not your mode. And don't isolate if talking would help. Know your mode and honor it.

**5. Track the waves.**
Logging your mood during grief isn't about self-improvement — it's about seeing that the waves do pass. On a bad day, looking at the data and seeing that last week had a 6 among the 3s is a reminder that the waves aren't constant. There are gaps.

**6. Get support.**
Grief is not meant to be processed alone. Whether it's friends, family, a support group, or a therapist — find someone who can hold space without trying to fix it.

*This is not a diagnosis. If grief is prolonged, complicated, or you're having thoughts of self-harm, please see a qualified professional immediately.*
