## Week 7 — Solution concept development

### Goal

Develop a testable solution concept by:
- Blueprinting a value-adding process.
- Diverging into components.
- Converging via clustering + SCAMPER.
- Documenting assumptions/uncertainties for Week 8 prototyping.

### Phase I (teamwork)

#### Step 1 — Select a value-adding process to blueprint

- Return to compiled insights and business context (Week 6).
- Review value-added processes associated with the primary HMW.
- Create/copy sticky notes representing three processes.
- As a team, select one process to blueprint.
- If unsure, select based on an assumption; document assumptions and uncertainties.

#### Step 2 — Summarize steps in the process

Summarize, at a high level, the value-added steps required for the selected process.

#### Step 3 — Blueprint three most value-added steps

- Blueprint 3 steps from the summarized process.
- Steps do not need to be contiguous.
- Focus on the most value-added steps.
- Objective: deep dive to uncover innovation opportunities.

#### Step 4 — Solo brainstorm solution components

- Return to the team blueprint.
- Identify innovation opportunities across swim lanes and steps.
- Generate solution components (blue-sky divergence).
- Strategy: reduce complexity through decomposition.

#### Step 5 — Team: cluster components into feasible solutions

- Synthesize divergent components from each team member.
- Cluster and combine into multiple feasible solutions.
- Be prepared to iterate.
- For each clustered solution, document:
  - Assumptions.
  - Unanswered questions.

### Phase III (teamwork, after Week 7 live session)

#### Step 6 — Review feedback and revise HMW

- Each team member reviews teaching team feedback on HMW questions.
- Team discusses and synthesizes reflections.
- Revise selected HMW or select a different HMW.

#### Step 7 — Select one feasible solution cluster to SCAMPER

- Select one clustered solution from Step 5.
- List all components in the selected solution.
- Apply SCAMPER to each component:
  - Substitute
  - Combine
  - Adapt
  - Modify
  - Put to use
  - Eliminate
  - Reverse

#### Step 8 — Document assumptions

Document:
- Remaining unanswered questions about the SCAMPERed solution.
- Aspects you are most unsure about.

## Week 8 — Storyboard prototype + experimentation plan

### Goal

Create a storyboard prototype and an experimentation plan to test key assumptions/uncertainties from Week 7 (and earlier).

### Week 8 milestone

- Complete Steps 7–10 and export as a single PDF for peer review submission (Blackboard) by Sunday of Week 8.

### Phase I — Planning your storyboard prototype

#### Step 1 — Purpose and scope of prototype

- Use your SCAMPER solution outcome to isolate what you believe are the customer’s most value-added steps.
- Review assumptions and unanswered questions documented in Week 7 and earlier.
- Copy/paste them into Week 8 team sandbox.
- Select which assumptions/uncertainties are most important to answer.

#### Step 2 — Testable hypotheses (2–3)

- Convert selected assumptions/uncertainties into 2–3 testable hypotheses.
- A testable hypothesis is an assertion that can be supported or refuted through testing.

#### Step 3 — Goals of testing

- Identify what feedback could support or refute each hypothesis.
- This feedback becomes the goal of the experimentation plan.

#### Step 4 — Establish details

Decide how much detail the storyboard needs to convey its purpose and elicit needed feedback.

Checklist:
- People
- Objects
- Location
- Interactions

#### Step 5 — Sketch a sample storyboard panel

- Choose a storyboard tool.
- Storyboard-specific software is not required (free-hand drawings are acceptable).

#### Step 6 — Rough draft storyboard

- Recommended to draft by Tuesday of Week 8 to maximize the Week 8 live session.

### Phase III — Storyboard + experimentation plan (Steps 7–10)

#### Step 7 — Storyboard context

Provide context for peer reviewers.

Include:
- POV statement (from Week 2).
- Revised HMW question (from Week 7 / Weeks 6–7).
- Background on how the storyboard was developed.

#### Step 8 — Storyboard

- Render storyboard for testing.
- Identify audience for testing (one of the team personas; individual or group appropriate to hypotheses).

#### Step 9 — Qualitative feedback plan

- Decide qualitative questions to be answered by the prototype.
- Have at least one qualitative question per hypothesis.
- Identify audience.
- Plan how qualitative data will be analyzed.

#### Step 10 — Quantitative feedback plan

- Decide quantitative questions to be answered by the prototype.
- Have at least one quantitative question per hypothesis.
- Identify audience.
- Plan how quantitative data will be analyzed.

## Week 9 — Customer Value Proposition (CVP)

### Context

Week 9 shifts from a user-centric design perspective to a customer-centric business creation perspective.

### Solo steps overview

#### Step 1 — Define your organization

- Establish corporate identity:
  - Strategy
  - Culture
  - Brand
- Choose innovation pathway (startup vs incumbent; explore/exploit choices).

#### Step 2 — Turn a novel solution into a complete product/service

- Start from user requirements (novel solution developed in Diamond 2).
- Identify paying customer and define Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD).
- Include relevant stakeholders (users, buyers, institutional stakeholders) as needed.

#### Step 3 — Craft your customer value proposition

Use a 6-step CVP approach:
- Customer Profile:
  - Select target customers.
  - Describe customer context.
  - List key JTBD.
  - Identify/prioritize pains and gains.
- Value Map:
  - Define/prioritize pain relievers and gain creators.
  - Articulate complete product/service description.
- Assess fit between Customer Profile and Value Map; adjust as needed.

#### Step 4 — Reflect and document assumptions

- List most important assumptions required for the CVP.
- Identify uncertainties/unanswered questions, especially related to the pivot from user-centered to customer-centered thinking.
