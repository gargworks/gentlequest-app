## PROJECT OVERVIEW

### Module Context
- **Duration:** Weeks 2–11 (10-week intensive project)

### **WEEK 3: Field Research Plan**

#### Research Objectives
- Validate POV assumptions
- Understand user pain points, workarounds, and aspirations
- Identify unmet needs in depth
- Discover adjacent problems and opportunities

#### Research Methods (Choose Multiple)
1. **Interviews (1:1 depth)**
   - Open-ended discovery
   - Probe on feelings, behaviors, context
   
2. **Observations**
   - Watch users in their natural environment
   - Note emotional states, decision points, friction
   
3. **Contextual Inquiry**
   - Follow users through their actual problem scenario
   - Document workflows, information gaps, critical moments
   
4. **Diary Studies**
   - User-generated records over time
   - Capture patterns, triggers, emotional arcs
   
5. **Co-Creation / Brainstorm Sessions**
   - Group ideation with potential users
   - Rapid solution sketching

#### Participant Sampling
- **Extreme Users (2 types):** Highest pain, lowest engagement
- **Diverse Users:** Different backgrounds, contexts, usage patterns
- **Target User Archetypes:** Build 3–5 representative personas

#### Documentation & Recording
- **With Consent:** Audio/video capture for reference
- **Synthesis Tools:** Affinity mapping, quote extraction, insight clustering
- **Bias Reflection:** Document researcher assumptions and blind spots

### **WEEKS 4–5: Research Execution & Persona Development**

#### Research Execution
- Conduct 12–15 interviews minimum
- Document 5–8 observations/contextual inquiries
- Synthesize 50+ insights from raw data

#### Persona Development Framework

**For Each Persona:**

| Attribute | Details |
|-----------|---------| 
| **Name & Avatar** | Memorable, realistic persona |
| **Age & Context** | Demographic, academic level, living situation |
| **Goals** | What they want to achieve (academic, social, health) |
| **Frustrations** | What prevents goal achievement |
| **Behaviors** | How they currently cope/seek help |
| **Motivations** | What drives their actions |
| **Barriers** | Stigma, cost, access, knowledge gaps |
| **Environment** | School, dorm, home, support network |

**Example Personas for Mental Health Project:**

**Persona 1: Overwhelmed High-Achiever (University Student)**
- Age: 20, competitive STEM major
- Goal: Maintain GPA while managing stress
- Frustration: Can't sleep due to workload; worried about seeking help damaging academic reputation
- Barrier: Stigma about mental health; expensive therapy
- Motivator: Career success, peer respect

**Persona 2: Isolated New Student**
- Age: 18, first-year college
- Goal: Fit in, build community, adjust to independence
- Frustration: Lonely; doesn't know where to start getting help
- Barrier: Unknown resources; fear of judgment
- Motivator: Feeling safe, belonging

**Persona 3: Concerned School Counselor**
- Role: School-based mental health practitioner
- Goal: Early detection and prevention; reduce student crisis incidents
- Frustration: Limited time, outdated screening tools, students reluctant to open up
- Barrier: Lack of literacy among peers; admin burden
- Motivator: Student wellbeing, measurable outcomes

#### ANRUM Insight Extraction
**ANRUM = Attitude, Need, Response, Use case, Mental model**

For each research artifact (quote, observation, incident):
- **Attitude:** What emotion/belief surfaced?
- **Need:** What unmet need does this reveal?
- **Response:** How did the user currently respond?
- **Use case:** What specific scenario triggered this?
- **Mental model:** What assumption does the user hold?

### **WEEKS 6–7: Ideation & Prototyping**

#### Ideation Framework: "How Might We...?" (HMW)

**HMW Template for Mental Health Project:**
- How might we make mental health resources discoverable without stigma?
- How might we enable early detection by peers (friends, roommates)?
- How might we provide affordable counseling alternatives?
- How might we gamify wellness habits (sleep, exercise, social connection)?
- How might we create safe, anonymous support communities?

#### Ideation Rules (Divergence)
1. Defer judgment
2. Encourage wild ideas
3. Build on others' ideas
4. Stay focused on one conversation
5. Be visual
6. Go for quantity

#### Prototype Types
- **Wireframes/Sketches:** User flow, navigation
- **Storyboards:** Scenario walkthroughs
- **Clickable Prototypes:** Figma/Balsamiq, basic interaction
- **Role-Play/Bodystorming:** Simulate user experience
- **Minimum Viable Product (MVP) Demo:** Coded proof of concept

#### Prototype Testing Loop
```
Prototype → Test with Users → Gather Feedback → Iterate
```

**Weeks 6–7 Milestone:** Prototype(s) + Feedback Summary (Blackboard)

### **WEEKS 8–9: Customer Value Proposition (CVP) Definition**

#### CVP Framework

A strong CVP articulates **what** you're solving, **for whom**, and **why it matters** in business terms.

| Component | Description | Mental Health Example |
|-----------|-------------|----------------------|
| **Customer Segment** | Who is your paying/primary user? | University students; school administrators; mental health providers |
| **Jobs to be Done (JTBD)** | What functional/emotional/social job do they need done? | "Manage stress without judgment"; "Detect at-risk students early"; "Provide care at scale" |
| **Value Proposition** | Specific benefits you offer | Early access to anonymous support; peer connections; gamified wellness tracking |
| **Pains** | Barriers users face today | Cost, stigma, information gaps, limited availability |
| **Gains** | Benefits users desire | Peace of mind, improved academics, prevention of crisis, community |
| **Pain Relievers** | How your solution eases pains | Affordable/free, anonymous, AI-guided, scalable, available 24/7 |
| **Gain Creators** | How your solution enables gains | Peer support, expert guidance, data-driven insights, gamification |

#### JTBD Decomposition (for Students)

| Job Layer | Details |
|-----------|---------| 
| **Functional** | Get fast, reliable access to mental health support; understand own mental state; manage stress/anxiety |
| **Emotional** | Feel less alone; reduce shame/stigma; regain control; have hope |
| **Social** | Maintain peer relationships; avoid judgment; be part of community |

#### Trade-Offs & Constraints
- **Accessibility vs. Privacy:** How much data collection for personalization?
- **Cost vs. Sustainability:** Free or subscription model?
- **Professional vs. Peer Support:** Combination approach?
- **Scale vs. Quality:** AI screening vs. human expertise?

#### Competitive Positioning
- **Direct:** Other mental health apps (Headspace, Calm, BetterHelp)
- **Indirect:** Therapy, support groups, school counselors
- **Non-consumption:** Students avoiding help entirely
- **Your differentiation:** [To be defined by team]
