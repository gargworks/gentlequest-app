### Persona 1: Experienced School Counselors

**Distinguished Characteristics:**
- School counselors with 5–10 years of experience handling large caseloads
- Highly organized and dedicated to student well-being, but often face burnout due to emotional demands
- Passionate about providing long-term support but constrained by limited resources and time

**Usage Scenario:**
In a typical day, these counselors manage multiple one-on-one and group counseling sessions. They are often frustrated by the need to manually track student progress over time, which makes it difficult to identify trends and provide targeted support. They struggle with managing large caseloads while ensuring each student receives adequate attention, especially when new issues arise unexpectedly.

**Key Tasks:**
1. Facilitating individual and group counseling sessions
2. Maintaining detailed student records and tracking progress over time
3. Coordinating with external mental health professionals to provide comprehensive support

**Key Frustrations:**
- Overwhelming caseloads, leading to less time for individual students
- Emotional fatigue from handling complex and challenging cases without enough support
- Bureaucratic delays in accessing necessary external resources for students

**Key Needs:**
- An integrated digital platform for tracking student progress and facilitating collaboration with external professionals
- Emotional support and training to manage burnout and emotional exhaustion
- Streamlined systems to reduce administrative burdens and allow more time for direct student interaction

**Supporting Evidence/Quotations:**
> "We need better tools to keep track of each student's journey. It's heartbreaking when a student falls through the cracks because we didn't have enough time to follow up."

**What This Persona Struggled With:**
- Managing a large number of students simultaneously while lacking an efficient system for tracking and documenting progress
- Some students not receiving timely follow-up care due to overwhelming workload
- Bureaucratic delays in accessing external mental health resources adding to frustration

**Workarounds/Adaptations:**
- Counselors develop their own tracking methods using spreadsheets or notebooks to manage student data
- Rely on informal networks of teachers for updates on student behavior, though this process is inefficient and time-consuming

**What Seems Most Important to This Persona:**
- Feeling they can make a tangible difference in students' lives
- Collaboration with teachers and parents, especially when it leads to positive changes in a student's mental health

**Overall Patterns:**
- A consistent theme of emotional investment in their role, paired with the frustration of inadequate support systems
- They desire tools that would allow them to focus more on counseling rather than administrative tasks

### Persona 2: Supportive High School Teachers

**Distinguished Characteristics:**
- Teachers with 10–15 years of experience who are strong advocates for student mental health
- Highly observant and empathetic, often identifying early signs of distress in students
- Committed to creating a safe and inclusive environment in their classrooms but feel underprepared for handling serious mental health concerns

**Usage Scenario:**
These teachers frequently notice changes in student behavior during class and initiate conversations to offer support. However, they often feel unequipped to handle deeper mental health challenges and rely on referrals to school counselors. They collaborate closely with school counselors when needed but find that time constraints limit their ability to provide consistent support.

**Key Tasks:**
1. Monitoring student well-being through classroom interactions and behaviors
2. Initiating conversations with students showing signs of distress and referring them to appropriate resources
3. Balancing academic responsibilities with the need to provide emotional support in the classroom

**Key Frustrations:**
- Limited time for meaningful one-on-one interactions with students
- Lack of formal training in mental health, making it difficult to provide the right support
- Frustration with the lack of parental engagement in mental health discussions, which sometimes hinders student progress

**Key Needs:**
- Access to training programs focused on early mental health intervention
- Better collaboration tools with school counselors to ensure students receive timely and comprehensive support
- Resources and strategies to engage parents in discussions about student mental health

**Supporting Evidence/Quotations:**
> "I see when students are struggling, but I don't always have the tools to help them."
> 
> "Parents often think it's just a phase, but we see the impact in the classroom every day."

**What This Persona Struggled With:**
- The biggest struggle is balancing teaching responsibilities with providing emotional support to students
- They feel they lack the expertise and time to address mental health issues effectively, leading to a sense of helplessness

**Workarounds/Adaptations:**
- Teachers often create informal support systems in their classrooms, such as quiet spaces or mindfulness activities
- They try to foster a more open environment to encourage students to express their feelings, even though they may not have formal training

**What Seems Most Important to This Persona:**
- Witnessing positive behavioral changes in students
- Knowing they have created a safe space for students to express themselves
- Collaboration with counselors when they feel it is effective

**Overall Patterns:**
- A strong desire to help and support students, but limited by time constraints and lack of training
- Teachers are often in need of more professional development to better support their students' mental health

### Solo Reflection

**Assessment of Data Quality:**
During the field research, I found that while the data gathered was insightful, there were certain limitations. Some of the insights were based on a small sample size, which may not fully capture the breadth of experiences among school counselors and teachers. For example, some perspectives on the challenges of engaging parents might vary significantly across different schools and regions. To ensure the reliability of our findings, additional data from a broader range of schools and mental health professionals could provide a more well-rounded view of the unmet needs.

**Attribution Bias:**
One challenge I faced was the tendency to perceive the observations from my own interviews as more critical than those of my teammates. This might be due to a bias towards valuing insights I directly gathered. To counter this, I will make a conscious effort to consider all team members' data equally during our convergence discussions. This will help us to synthesize a more comprehensive understanding of the user needs without overemphasizing any one viewpoint.

**Assumptions:**
In developing the personas, I assumed that the emotional toll of handling large caseloads is a universal experience among school counselors, but this may not be true for all regions or schools with varying resources. Similarly, while I assumed that teachers often feel undertrained in handling mental health issues, this might not hold for those who have received specific mental health training. It is crucial to clarify these assumptions with my team so that we can adjust our findings if our collective data reveals different trends.

**Overall Reflection:**
The field research highlighted some important strengths, such as the genuine passion of educators and counselors for supporting students' well-being. It also revealed a significant gap in training and resources, which aligns with our initial hypothesis about the needs of these groups. As we move into the convergence phase, I believe our focus should be on balancing our individual findings to ensure that our final insights are both representative and actionable.

### Field Observations — Key Challenges Identified

**Stigma & Confidentiality Concerns:**
- Students fear being stigmatized or judged if they seek help for mental health issues

**Resource Limitations:**
- Lack of specialized mental health providers and resources within educational institutions

**Fragmented Services:**
- Hard for students to navigate and access the appropriate care

**Identification and Diagnosis:**
- Symptoms may be subtle or mistaken for normal stress and anxiety related to academic pressures

**Time Constraints:**
- Not enough practitioners

### Field Observations — Value of Mental Health Support

**Long-term Well-being:**
- Early and effective intervention can improve long-term mental health and well-being, helping students to thrive both academically and personally

**Early Identification and Treatment:**
- Early detection of mental health issues can prevent them from worsening

**Personalized Care:**
- Mental health practitioners can provide personalized care tailored to the specific needs of each student

**Confidentiality and Professionalism:**
- Practitioners are bound by confidentiality, which can help students feel safe and secure in sharing their concerns without fear of judgment

**Improved Academic Performance:**
- Addressing mental health issues can lead to better concentration, improved academic performance

**Support and Coping Strategies:**
- Practitioners can offer support and teach coping strategies to help students manage stress, anxiety, and other mental health challenges

**Reduced Stigma:**
- Seeking professional help can contribute to reducing the stigma associated with mental health issues, encouraging more students to seek the help they need

### POEMS Framework Analysis (Parents)

**People:**
- **Parents:** Stressed, worried, sense of helplessness, overwhelmed by the responsibility of supporting their child's mental health
- **Child:** Struggles with mental health symptoms such as anxiety, depression, behavioral issues; feels misunderstood or isolated
- **Healthcare Professionals:** Provide support and treatment but may also face challenges in communicating effectively with both the parent and child

**Objects:**
- **Medication:** Prescribed to manage symptoms but is a source of concern for parents regarding side effects and long-term use
- **Therapeutic Tools:** Journals, stress balls, art supplies used in therapy sessions to help the child express their feelings

**Environment:**
- **Home:** Safe and supportive space, but can sometimes become a place of tension and conflict
- **School:** A critical environment where the child faces additional stressors or support, depending on the school's resources and understanding of mental health
- **Healthcare Settings:** Clinics or hospitals where the child receives treatment; these environments can be both a source of relief and anxiety for the parent and child

**Messages:**
- **Communication:** The importance of open, honest, and supportive communication between the parent, child, and healthcare professionals
- **Stigma:** Addressing and overcoming the stigma associated with mental health issues to ensure the child feels supported and understood

**Services:**
- **Therapy:** Regular sessions with a psychologist or counselor to help the child manage their symptoms
- **Support Groups:** For both parents and children, providing a community of understanding and shared experiences

### Key Patterns Observed

- Parents were experiencing high levels of stress and anxiety, worrying about their child's well-being and future
- It can be difficult to maintain open and effective communication with a child who is struggling with mental health issues
- The process of seeking and accessing mental health services is often complex and emotionally taxing
- The journey involves significant stress for both the parent and the child, along with moments of hope and relief when progress is made

## Template (guide)

  - **Backstage**
  - **Profit formula**
- Convert assumptions into testable hypotheses

## Team-filled (project data)

The export includes a Week 11 MVE area plus reflection prompts tied to the DD (Double Diamond) cycles and assumption tracking.

### Key Assumptions to Test (by category)

#### Frontstage (Desirability)

**Assumption 1:** Students will engage regularly with the app (gamification, self-assessment, community features)
- **Risk:** Low engagement → insufficient data for AI personalization
- **Experiment:** Beta test with 50 high school students for 4 weeks; track daily/weekly active users
- **Metric:** % of users who log in at least 3x/week
- **Threshold:** > 60% of users meet engagement threshold → proceed; < 40% → pivot on gamification/UX

**Assumption 2:** Caregivers/schools want real-time progress updates
- **Risk:** Stakeholders may not value real-time updates (prefer periodic reports)
- **Experiment:** Interviews with 10 school counselors + 10 parents; prototype dashboard walkthrough
- **Metric:** % who say real-time updates are "very important" or "critical"
- **Threshold:** > 70% → proceed; < 50% → adjust update frequency

#### Backstage (Feasibility)

**Assumption 3:** AI can deliver accurate, personalized mental health recommendations
- **Risk:** AI recommendations may not be accurate or actionable for diverse student needs
- **Experiment:** Pilot AI engine with 30 students; have licensed counselors review AI recommendations for accuracy
- **Metric:** % of AI recommendations rated "accurate and helpful" by counselors
- **Threshold:** > 75% → proceed; < 60% → refine AI model

**Assumption 4:** Platform can integrate with existing school systems (e.g., student information systems)
- **Risk:** Technical integration may be complex, costly, or incompatible
- **Experiment:** Technical feasibility study with 3 schools; API integration test
- **Metric:** # of schools where integration is feasible within budget/timeline
- **Threshold:** 2+ out of 3 → proceed; 0–1 → pivot to standalone platform

#### Profit Formula (Viability)

**Assumption 5:** Schools/institutions will pay subscription fees (e.g., $10–$20 per student/year)
- **Risk:** Schools may not have budget or willingness to pay
- **Experiment:** Pricing surveys + LOI (letters of intent) from 10 schools
- **Metric:** # of schools willing to sign LOI at proposed price point
- **Threshold:** 5+ LOIs → proceed; < 3 → adjust pricing or seek alternative revenue model (freemium, grants)

**Assumption 6:** Customer acquisition cost (CAC) is sustainable relative to lifetime value (LTV)
- **Risk:** Cost to acquire schools/students may exceed revenue
- **Experiment:** Run pilot marketing campaign (digital ads, school outreach) for 3 months; track CAC vs. LTV
- **Metric:** LTV:CAC ratio
- **Threshold:** Ratio > 3:1 → proceed; < 2:1 → pivot on marketing/channel strategy
