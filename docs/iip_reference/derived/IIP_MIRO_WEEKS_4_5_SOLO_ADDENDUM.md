## Team Member 3: Victoria Tan — Personas + Field Observations (additional details)

### Method framing (captured)
- **Day in the Life (Parents):** daily interactions/challenges in managing children’s mental health
- **Shadowing (Students):** patterns in stress, isolation, engagement, and social dynamics

### Persona 1: Mothers with young adult children (example: Lisa)

**Distinguished characteristics (captured):**
- Caring, proactive, seeks best practices
- Feels urgency/responsibility

**Context / scenario (captured):**
- “Lisa, a single mother” managing her son “Max’s anxiety”
- Observations span a typical day (morning routine → school drop-off → work → evening routine → bedtime)

**Key daily observations (captured):**
- Lisa uses affirmations and helps Max plan tasks, but feels he needs specialized support
- Max shows reluctance to face classes; stress about deadlines and social interactions
- Lisa checks phone often at work, seeks reassurance/updates
- Lisa feels there are not enough support mechanisms; she’s “at a loss at where or whom she can turn to for professional help”
- Teachers have limited time to discuss Max’s issues, leaving Lisa unsure where to turn

**Key needs (captured):**
- Immediate and effective mental health resources for young people
- Better support systems and clearer pathways for parents seeking help

**Supporting quotations (captured):**
- “Despite our best efforts, it feels like we’re always on our own.”
- “It’s overwhelming trying to navigate all this mostly on our own.”
- “I feel like we’re always waiting for something or someone that might help.”

### Persona 2: High school students (example: Emily, 17)

**Distinguished characteristics (captured):**
- Introverted, experiences social anxiety, prefers structured environments

**School-day observation themes (captured):**
- Peer interactions often surface-level; students feel overlooked in large class sizes
- Disengagement in group projects when tasks are not evenly distributed
- Teacher overload limits ability to provide individual attention
- Awareness of counselor exists but stigma reduces utilization
- Mental health resources within school described as minimal

**User journey fragments (captured):**
- Arrival → first period math → lunch break → group project → leaving school
- Emotional states include apprehensive, overwhelmed, stressed, tired/relieved

**Key frustrations (captured):**
- Overwhelming unstructured environments (e.g., cafeteria)
- Unclear group project roles and feeling overlooked
- Academic pressure in competitive environment

**Key needs (captured):**
- Structured social interactions and supportive, understanding teachers
- Clear communication and defined roles in group projects
- Accessible, stigma-reducing ways to seek help

**Supporting quotations (captured):**
- “Sometimes, it feels like everything is just too much, and I don’t know how to handle it on my own.”
- “I hope I can find someone to talk to about how I’m feeling, someone who really understands and can help me figure things out.”

**Isolated insights + updated POV (captured):**
- Parents: need easier access to mental health resources; feel overwhelmed by lack of services and isolated navigating transition-to-adulthood support
- Students: need less-overwhelming school settings; struggle with social anxiety and feel overlooked, reducing engagement and learning

**Assumptions (captured):**
- Parents: assumes accessibility/availability correlates directly to better outcomes (may ignore quality/fit/capacity factors)
- Students: assumes structured environments reduce anxiety (may ignore mental health history or external stressors)

**Distinguished characteristics (captured):**

### Persona 2: S. Zhang (University student seeking support)

**Distinguished characteristics (captured):**

- Faces pressure: studies, relationship confusion, peer/family pressure

**Key frustrations (captured):**
