## IV. The Satellite View (Multi-Dimensional Navigation)

### From Part 2:
> "Think of it like electricity lighting up certain parts of the brain in beautiful patterns. We take a photo of that, then activate another part, lighting up that part of the world. We want to light up this whole world into a beautiful harmony of light - like Google satellite view where you see the planet or continent, but can also zoom into a city and see lights emerge. That multi-dimensional ability to navigate through full context - a Neuralink for coding."

**The Four Dimensions:**

1. **Zoom Levels:** Continent (vision) → Country (sprint) → City (session) → Street (edit)
2. **Time:** See work light up over weeks (Google Maps timeline)
3. **Neural Pathways:** Preserve "lit patterns" when switching contexts
4. **Thought → Production:** Brain → Voice → AI → Code → Validation (seamless)

**Immutable Principle:**
> **Work is not linear tasks. It's multi-dimensional patterns across zoom, time, and thought pathways. The system must navigate this, not flatten it.**

## V. Session Wrapping (Expansion/Contraction)

### From Part 2:
> "Whatever we started in the session should wrap up in the session itself. But don't rush me with 'Should we stop now?' Be aware we're zooming in or out. Preserve pathways when switching."

**The Pattern:**
1. **Expand:** Explore, diverge, light up pathways
2. **Contract:** Wrap something tangible before ending
3. **Preserve:** Save the lit pattern for next time

**Anti-Patterns:**
- ❌ "Should we wrap up now?"
- ❌ Rushing to completion
- ❌ Forcing premature task closure

**Immutable Principle:**
> **Sessions must reach tangible progress, but the system shouldn't pressure the user to stop. Preserve neural pathways, don't lose electricity.**

## VI. Validation Hierarchy (Localhost → Production → Users)

### From Part 1:
> "Done is tiered, not binary. We should know at what level we are so expectations are set properly."

**The Tiers:**

| Level | Trigger | Confidence | Frequency |
|:------|:--------|:-----------|:----------|
| **Street (Localhost)** | Widget appears, tests pass | Low | Per-feature |
| **City (Production)** | Working on Render | High | Daily/session |
| **Country (App Store)** | Live on app stores | Highest | Weekly/sprint |
| **Continent (Users)** | Real user feedback | Ultimate | Ongoing |

**Immutable Principle:**
> **The system must understand context and validate at the appropriate tier. Don't force full testing at every level - it kills momentum.**

## XVI. Brain Consolidation (The Meta-Layer)

### From Meta-Observation (2026-01-04):
> "Like the brain consolidates memory during sleep, we should periodically consolidate, restructure, and refactor artifacts. Are we just accumulating or are we also pruning neurons?"

**The Two-Phase Memory:**

| Phase | What | When | How |
|:------|:-----|:-----|:----|
| **RAM (Capture)** | Instant, unfiltered capture | During sessions | Create MDs immediately |
| **Sleep (Consolidate)** | Restructure, merge, prune | Background (CEO) | Automated cleanup |

**The Problem:** 30+ MD files created during workflow design. Risk of artifact sprawl and cognitive overload.

**The Solution:**
1. **Capture fast** (don't lose context during active sessions)
2. **Consolidate later** (merge redundant docs, prune outdated, strengthen links)
3. **Automate it** (CEO handles consolidation, Chairman approves conflicts)

**Examples of Consolidation:**
- Merge 4 RAW_MONOLOGUE files into consolidated doc
- Archive completed implementation plans
- Update North Star with newly discovered principles
- Prune duplicate synthesis docs after merging

**Immutable Principle:**
> **The system must manage its own artifacts. Capture is RAM (fast, unfiltered). Consolidation is sleep (background, automated). The CEO runs this consolidation loop.**

**Applies Beyond MDs:**
- Task states (prune completed tasks)
- Event ledger (archive old events)

## XVII. Honest Time Estimation (Agentic vs Human)

### From Feedback (2026-01-05):
> "You said it will take days and you did it in half an hour. You should know how important this is and what kind of detail you need to go through."

**The Problem:** AI gives human-scale estimates ("Week 1-4") when work is actually agentic-scale (hours).

**Why This Happens:**
- "Week 1" sounds reasonable to humans
- But agentic work is parallelizable, doesn't sleep, no context switching
- What takes a human team 1 week might take AI 2-4 hours of focused work

**The Damage:**
- User prepares for long effort
- Work completes in hours
- Creates confusion about scope/importance
- Undermines trust in estimates

**Immutable Principle:**
> **Always estimate in AGENTIC HOURS, not human weeks. Be specific about the actual effort level:**
> - "2-4 hours of focused agentic work" ✅
> - "Week 1" when you mean 2-4 hours ❌

**Calibration Guide:**

| Human Estimate | Agentic Reality | Example |
|:---------------|:----------------|:--------|
| "1 week" | 6-10 hours | Full feature with tests |
| "1 day" | 2-4 hours | Single component |
| "Half day" | 30-60 min | Simple script or config |
| "1 hour" | 5-15 min | Trivial change |

**When Uncertain:**
- State uncertainty: "This could be 2 hours or 8, depending on edge cases"
- Break into phases: "Core logic: 2 hours. Polish: 2-4 more hours"
- Ask for detail level: "Should this be production-grade (8 hours) or prototype (2 hours)?"

**Why This Matters:**
- User allocates mental energy based on estimates
- Underestimating makes work seem trivial
- Overestimating creates false sense of complexity
- **Honest estimation builds trust**
