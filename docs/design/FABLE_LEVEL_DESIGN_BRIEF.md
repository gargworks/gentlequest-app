# GentleQuest — Fable-Level Design Brief

> Design what no competitor will dare — because they optimize for engagement metrics, not for the person in a low moment at 2 AM.
> Every pixel should make them feel held, not measured.

## 1. The Companion as Emotional Architecture

Finch's bird is a Tamagotchi — it gets sad if you don't check in. That's manipulation dressed as care. GentleQuest's companion is the **anti-Tamagotchi**: a living thing that waits without suffering when you're gone.

- **The "return after absence" moment.** Not "welcome back!" — that's guilt-adjacent. The companion is exactly where you left it. The plant didn't wilt. The soil is the same. The breathing animation never stopped. Design this as a **deliberate non-event** — the most powerful moment in the app is the one where nothing changed, because everything else in their life did.
- **The companion's presence in chat.** Not a header avatar — a **silent witness**. When the user types something heavy, the companion shifts (not reacts — shifts). When the user is in crisis, the companion doesn't disappear or get urgent — it stays, quietly. Design the micro-animations that communicate "I'm here" without saying it.
- **Growth as landscape, not level bar.** Stage 5 isn't "mature tree = max level." It's a **grove** — the companion's space grows, not the creature. More soil, more sky, more quiet. The progression is environmental, not hierarchical. No one has done this.

## 2. The Crisis Path as Sacred Architecture

Every ADHD app treats crisis as a liability screen — a 988 button, a modal, a legal checkbox. Fable treats it as the **most important design moment in the product**.

- **The crisis transition.** When the AI detects crisis language, design the **transition itself** — how the chat slows down, how the companion moves to the foreground, how 988 appears not as a popup but as a **door that was always there**. The crisis path should feel like the app was designed for this moment from the first pixel.
- **The "after" — what happens when crisis passes.** No app does this. The user tapped 988, or didn't. They come back. What do they see? Not "How are you feeling?" — that's tone-deaf. Design the **re-entry surface**: the companion is there, the chat history is intact, no banner saying "you were in crisis." The app remembers without labeling.

## 3. The Anti-Dashboard

Every mental health app shows charts, scores, trends, streaks. Fable kills the dashboard.

- **The weekly review as a letter, not a report.** Not "You logged 5 times, average mood 3.2." Instead: a **prose reflection** in the app's voice, referencing specific moments. "Tuesday was hard. You walked at lunch anyway. That mattered." The data drives the prose; the user never sees a number. The shareable card becomes a **fragment of that letter** — not a chart, a sentence.
- **The "no data" state as beautiful.** When someone hasn't logged all week, the weekly review isn't empty or shaming. It's a **single line**: "You were living. That counts." With the companion, unchanged. Absence is not a gap in the data — it's a valid state.

## 4. Body Doubling as Presence, Not Productivity

The roadmap says body doubling is the wedge. The current implementation is a timer. Fable designs it as **shared solitude**.

- **The body doubling session as a space, not a timer.** When you start, you don't see a countdown — you see a **shared space**: your companion, and a faint indication that others are also working (not who, not how many — just that you're not alone). The timer exists but is invisible unless you ask. The session ends not with a bell but with a **gentle return** — the space fades, the companion breathes, and you're back.
- **The "someone left" moment.** In live body doubling (Stage 2), when someone leaves mid-session, the app doesn't announce it. The space just gets slightly quieter. No "X has left." No count change. Presence without surveillance.

## 5. The Onboarding as a Promise, Not a Funnel

Current onboarding: welcome → age gate → compliance → chat. Functional. Fable designs it as a **covenant**.

- **The first 60 seconds as a vow.** Not "Welcome to GentleQuest!" but a **quiet contract**: "This is your companion. It will wait for you. It never punishes. What you say here stays here. If it gets bad, 988 is always one tap away — even offline." The companion seed appears. The user taps "Begin" — not "Get Started" or "Sign Up." The word matters. Design the typography, the pacing, the silence between lines. This is the moment that decides if they come back.

## Design Principles (existing, from REVIEW.md — still load-bearing)

P1 — Warmth over utility. P2 — Skip anything, no shame. P3 — One clear next action.
P4 — Amber, not red. P5 — Privacy is visible. P6 — Crisis never blocks.
P9 — Companion framing, not app framing. P14 — Gentle structure, not gamification.

## What makes this Fable-level

These designs can't be A/B tested into existence. They require a point of view about what care looks like when it's not performing. The competitor won't build them because:
- The "return after absence" non-event has no engagement metric
- The crisis "after" has no retention funnel
- The anti-dashboard has no chart to screenshot for investor decks
- Body doubling as space has no timer to push-optimize
- The onboarding vow has no conversion rate to optimize

That's exactly why they're the moat. Design them as if you're the last app this person opens tonight.
