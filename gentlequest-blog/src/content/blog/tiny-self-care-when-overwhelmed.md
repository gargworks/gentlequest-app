## When "self-care" feels like another task

You're overwhelmed. Your to-do list is growing. Your chest is tight. And some article is telling you to "practice self-care" — like that's one more thing to add to the list.

If self-care feels like homework, it's not self-care. It's another obligation.

This list is different. These are **2-minute steps** that require zero preparation, zero commitment, and zero streaks. You can do one and stop. You can do none and that's fine too.

No guilt. No "you should be doing more." Just small relief, if you want it.

## 1. Name what you're feeling (30 seconds)

Don't fix it. Don't analyze it. Just name it.

"I feel overwhelmed."
"I feel anxious."
"I feel stuck."

Naming an emotion reduces its intensity. It's called **affect labeling** — and neuroscience shows it activates the prefrontal cortex while dampening the amygdala. You don't need to solve the feeling. You just need to acknowledge it exists.

## 2. The 5-4-3-2-1 grounding technique (90 seconds)

When your mind is racing, pull it back to your body:

- **5** things you can see
- **4** things you can touch
- **3** things you can hear
- **2** things you can smell
- **1** thing you can taste

This isn't a magic cure. It's just a way to interrupt the spiral for 90 seconds. Sometimes 90 seconds is enough to catch your breath.

## 3. One stretch, anywhere (30 seconds)

Not a yoga routine. Not a workout. One stretch.

- Roll your shoulders back three times
- Reach for the ceiling
- Touch your toes (or as far as you can get)
- Twist your torso left, then right

Tension lives in your body when you're overwhelmed. One stretch won't fix everything, but it reminds your nervous system that you're here, in a body, and that body can move.

## 4. Cold water on your face (10 seconds)

Go to the bathroom. Splash cold water on your face.

This triggers the **mammalian dive reflex** — an evolutionary response that slows your heart rate and activates your parasympathetic nervous system. It's the fastest biological hack for "my heart is racing and I can't think."

If you can't splash water, hold something cold against your wrists or the back of your neck.

## 5. Write down one thing (60 seconds)

Not a journal entry. Not a brain dump. One thing.

One thing that's making you feel overwhelmed. One thing you accomplished today (even if it's "got out of bed"). One thing you're looking forward to. One thing you're worried about.

The act of writing externalizes the thought. It moves it from your head (where it loops) to a page (where it stays). You don't have to do anything with it. Just write it.

## The rule: no streaks

If you do one of these today and none tomorrow, that's fine. If you do none today, that's fine too. The goal isn't consistency — it's having tools available when you need them.

Streaks turn self-care into another thing you can fail at. We don't want that. You're already overwhelmed. The last thing you need is a wellness app making you feel guilty for not doing enough.
