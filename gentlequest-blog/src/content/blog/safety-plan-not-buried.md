Most mental health apps bury the safety plan.

It's in Settings. Under a submenu. Three taps deep. Behind "More." Next to
"Export data" and "About."

The logic is understandable — you don't want to scare new users. A safety plan
feels heavy. It implies crisis. So apps tuck it away, hoping you'll fill it in
during a calm moment and never need it.

But that logic breaks down in the moment you actually need it.

## The problem with buried safety plans

When you're in crisis, you're not navigating menus. You're not reading labels.
Your working memory is shot. Your hands might be shaking. You need the thing
that helps — right now, not in three taps.

A safety plan that takes 4 taps to reach is a safety plan that doesn't get used.

Research on crisis intervention consistently shows that **reducing friction**
is the single most effective design choice. The fewer steps between a person
and help, the more likely they are to get help. This is why suicide prevention
hotlines are 3 digits, not 10.

## Where we put it

In GentleQuest, the safety plan lives at the **top of your profile**.

Not in Settings. Not under "More." Not behind a lock screen. It's the first
thing you see when you tap your profile icon.

It includes:
- **Crisis helplines** for your country (auto-detected, editable)
- **People you trust** — names and numbers you fill in when calm
- **Things that help** — grounding techniques, music, places
- **Warning signs** — what to watch for in yourself

You fill it in once, when you're calm. You find it in one tap, when you're not.

## Fill it when you're calm. Find it when you're not.

That's the whole principle. The safety plan is not a feature you use every day.
It's a feature that exists for the worst day. And on the worst day, you don't
have the bandwidth to dig.

If you haven't filled one in yet, take 2 minutes now. Open the app → tap your
profile → tap "Safety Plan." Add one crisis number. Add one person. That's
enough to start.

You can always add more later. But having something — even one number — already
there when you need it is the difference between reaching out and not.
