Anxiety is a time traveler.

It lives in the future — in the what-ifs, the catastrophes that haven't
happened, the conversations you haven't had. Your body reacts as if those
things are happening right now. Heart rate up. Chest tight. Thoughts racing.

But you're not in the future. You're here. In a room. Reading this.

The 5-4-3-2-1 grounding exercise is a way to remind your nervous system of
that. It takes about 60 seconds. You don't need an app, a quiet room, or any
preparation. You can do it on a bus, at your desk, in a bathroom stall.

## How it works

Name these things, out loud or in your head:

1. **5 things you can see.** The wall. A cup. A crack in the ceiling. The
   color of someone's jacket. Don't interpret — just see.

2. **4 things you can touch.** The fabric of your sleeve. The hard edge of a
   table. The warmth of your phone. Your own feet on the floor.

3. **3 things you can hear.** A fan. Traffic. Your own breathing. Distant
   voices. Don't judge the sounds — just notice them.

4. **2 things you can smell.** Coffee. Rain. The faint smell of paper. If you
   can't smell anything, name two smells you remember.

5. **1 thing you can taste.** The inside of your mouth. A sip of water. Gum.

That's it. You're back.

## Why it works

Your brain can't fully inhabit two sensory modes at once. When you force it to
process real, present-moment sensory input — what you're actually seeing,
touching, hearing — it has less bandwidth for the imaginary future scenario
that's making you anxious.

This isn't a cure for anxiety. It's a circuit breaker. It doesn't solve the
problem causing the anxiety. It brings you back to a state where you can
think about the problem clearly, instead of reacting to a phantom.

## When to use it

- Before a stressful meeting or conversation
- When you can't fall asleep because your mind is racing
- During a panic attack
- When you notice your chest tightening and thoughts spiraling
- Anytime you feel "disconnected" or unreal

You don't need to wait for a full-blown crisis. The earlier you catch the
spiral, the easier it is to redirect.
